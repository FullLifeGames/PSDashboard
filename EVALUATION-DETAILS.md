# Evaluation details

This document holds the mechanisms behind [EVALUATION.md](./EVALUATION.md): the rules, thresholds and traps of the spread solver, the search, the score terms, the prover and the report. Read it when you change the engine; EVALUATION.md alone says what the engine does and what a number means.

Each section carries the heading of the section it extends in EVALUATION.md, in the same order.

## 1. What the app knows about a game

### Rule-outs

**The census of spent items.** [`inference/spent-items.ts`](./packages/replay-core/src/inference/spent-items.ts) finds its items by asking the Dex's handlers, so no item name stands in the code. `spentAfterOwnMove` lists the items of the replay's generation whose `onAfterMoveSecondarySelf` calls `useItem` or `eatItem` on a stand-in holder for some damaging move (Throat Spray, from Generation 8 and in Pokémon Champions); `spentByHit` does the same for `onAfterMoveSecondary`, `onDamagingHit` and `onHit` (Generation 4: Enigma, Jaboca and Rowap Berry; Generation 5 adds Absorb Bulb and Cell Battery; Generation 6 adds Kee Berry, Maranga Berry, Luminous Moss, Snowball and Weakness Policy; Generation 3 has none). Eject Button and Red Card fall out of the census on their own, because their handlers need a battle the stand-in lacks and a handler that throws proves nothing, and they keep their own rule. Both lists are cached per Dex. [`spent-items-sweep.spec.ts`](./packages/replay-core/test/spent-items-sweep.spec.ts) plays each item against the simulator in every generation and in Pokémon Champions, singles and doubles, and holds the census and every verdict to what the simulator does.

**The stand-in call.** `spendsAfterOwnMove` and `spendsOnHit` run the handler with a stand-in holder (1 HP, active, a `useItem` and an `eatItem` that record the spend), a stand-in attacker and a stand-in battle whose `damage`, `heal`, `boost` and `add` do nothing. The log's facts enter as the stand-ins' answers. `getMoveHitData` returns the effectiveness the log printed (neutral when it printed none), and `hasAbility` answers "may have", so a handler that refuses on an ability (Magic Guard against Jaboca Berry) refuses whenever the ability is possible. `eatItem` fails while a foe may have Unnerve or As One, and when the item's own `onTryEatItem` fails on a `TryHeal` that Heal Block blocks (Enigma Berry). The `onAfterMoveSecondary` handler does not run when the attacker may have Sheer Force and the move has secondaries, as in the simulator, while `onDamagingHit` and `onHit` do. Klutz, Embargo and Magic Room stop the item before the call, and so does Sheer Force for Throat Spray, through the same gates as Life Orb and Shell Bell (`ownItemActs` in [`inference/item-evidence.ts`](./packages/replay-core/src/inference/item-evidence.ts) for Throat Spray, `ignoresItem` in [`inference/item-triggers.ts`](./packages/replay-core/src/inference/item-triggers.ts) for a hit).

**The window of a hit.** The `-supereffective` and `-resisted` lines of a hit are noted per holder for the move in hand, and the next `move` line clears them. A spread move prints every effectiveness line before its damage lines, so the reading is per holder, never "the last line". A `-damage` on a holder from another Pokémon's move opens the rule beside Eject Button and Red Card, and the next action boundary judges it. A Substitute that takes the hit prints no `-damage` on the holder and opens nothing; a holder that faints in the hit drops its triggers; an Eject Button, Red Card or Eject Pack that spent itself in the window voids the judgment of the hit.

**The type and category gate.** `spendsOnHit` replays the stand-in with the move at every Dex type and in both categories; an item whose outcome changes reads the move's kind (Kee Berry reads a physical hit, Absorb Bulb a Water one). Such an item gets a verdict only when the kind cannot have shifted, and `kindMayShift` in `inference/item-triggers.ts` says when it may: a move the type of which the log may not match (`moveTypeMayShift` in [`inference/blocked-effects.ts`](./packages/replay-core/src/inference/blocked-effects.ts): Electrify or Ion Deluge this turn, a Z or Max move, a name that is not the Dex id, an `on*ModifyType` handler), a move with an `onModifyMove` handler (Shell Side Arm, Photon Geyser and Tera Blast flip the category there), or an attacker that may have an ability of `kindShifters` (an `on*ModifyType` handler, or an `onModifyMove` that changes the type of a sample move on a stand-in: Normalize, the -ate abilities, Liquid Voice). Weakness Policy, Enigma Berry and Throat Spray read neither type nor category and stay judged. A reading by the move's Dex type would rule Absorb Bulb out after a Normalize Water Gun, and one by its Dex category would rule Maranga Berry out after a Shell Side Arm that turned physical; the sweep holds both scenes.

**The Protective Pads tell.** `contactBreakers` in [`inference/contact-tell.ts`](./packages/replay-core/src/inference/contact-tell.ts) lists the legal items of the Dex (they exist, belong to the generation and carry no `isNonstandard`) for which `contactLands` is false while it is true with no item: Tackle gives Protective Pads from Generation 7, a punching move adds Punching Glove from Generation 9, and Pokémon Champions gives none, because its Dex marks Protective Pads as past. `judgeSilentHelmet` in `inference/item-evidence.ts` notes the tell when an opponent's action hit a holder the log showed with a Rocky Helmet, that holder printed no helmet line and did not faint, and the attacker's abilities are known and exclude Long Reach and Magic Guard. The holder cannot have Klutz and stands under neither Embargo nor Magic Room. The census rows of the sweep play every Dex item against Tackle and every Dex ability as attacker and as holder, and hold the two attacker abilities and the holder's Klutz to the simulator's own answers.

**The tell writer.** A tell is a guess the log's silence points to, so it never enters the Pokémon while the lines run: an item value set early would read as known and stop every later rule-out of that Pokémon. `noteItemTell` keeps the last tell noted for a Pokémon (the Pads tell and the Heavy-Duty Boots tell share it), and `applyItemTells` writes the guesses after the last line. It skips a Pokémon whose item a line named (a later Knock Off that shows the Pads included), an item the log ruled out for it, and an item that the format's Item Clause has used up. `itemClauseLimit` reads the limit from the log's `|rule|Item Clause: Limit N of each item` line, else from the tier's rule table; the items counted are those the side's Pokémon show (a consumed item too) plus the tells already written. The team builder's `itemTells` collects the guesses, and `resolveItem` plays them unless the item is ruled out.

### Usage and set guesses

The vetoes trim only guessed fills; revealed and manual knowledge is proof and is never second-guessed. A published set scores 2 points per move slot a revealed move fills, 2 for the revealed item and 2 for the revealed ability. A set the log rules out (its ability, or an item slot whose every listed item is ruled out) is disqualified. A set with fewer points than revealed moves contradicts the log and yields none, and the build then falls back to marginal assembly plus the vetoes. Ties break toward the set whose moves the usage marginals like best (the sum of log probabilities).

**The default ability.** The last link of the ability chain (known, curated, the revealed value, usage, the Smogon set) is `firstAllowedAbility` in [`team/dex-legal.ts`](./packages/replay-core/src/team/dex-legal.ts): the first of the species' slots 0, 1 and hidden that the log did not rule out and the format does not ban, and slot 0 when every slot is out (a wrong ability beats none). `formatAbilityBans(log)` supplies the bans, and a format's own usage and sets hold none, so only this link reads them. A tier that the simulator knows and that is no custom game gets its own `TeamValidator`: the bans are the abilities that `checkAbility` rejects, found once per format and process. A tier the simulator does not know (a draft league, a VGC regulation) and a custom game take their bans from the log's `|rule|Name: description` lines: one validator per rule the simulator knows as a validator rule, on the generation's custom game (the Champions custom game for the Champions mod), and the union of the rejected abilities. The route needs one validator per rule, because several rule names in one format string ban nothing: "Evasion Clause" contains "Evasion Abilities Clause", and the rule table then drops every custom rule. A line without a colon (a video watermark) names no rule, and a custom game without rule lines bans nothing. `dex-legal.spec.ts` plays every species of every corpus tier and of the draft rule lines against the validator's `checkAbility`.

### Fitted spreads and inferred Scarf

**Races.** A race is every opposite-side pair of the turn's movers in action order, the earlier mover having won it. A pair drops when the earlier mover is not clean as a first mover, the later one not as a second, or either slot was rearranged (After You and Quash mark their target for the turn). A Quick Claw, Quick Draw or Custap Berry activation makes the holder unclean as a first mover only. The parser reads contamination by direction at decision time: an observation drops only when the factor could explain the observed order, a speed-raising factor (Tailwind, a positive Speed stage, a paradox booster) on the first mover or a speed-lowering one (paralysis, a negative Speed stage) on the second. The other directions stay as implied constraints. Trick Room inverts the order and same-turn entries are unknowable at order time, so both stay bilateral. Paradox boosters are volatiles, not stages, and any variant counts as a raiser.

**Scarf bookkeeping.** The parser notes a Choice Scarf that comes or goes for its holder and its giver, because the solver reads a race against the set's item unless the order names the Scarf held. A line moves a Scarf when it removes one (Knock Off, a theft, the giver's side of a swap into nothing), when one arrives with its giver (`[of]`, else the other party of the pending Trick or Switcheroo) and when a known Scarf is swapped for another item; Frisk only reveals. The races of such a Pokémon drop in the turn it moved and read with the Scarf it held at every other race.

**The order limit.** An observed move order is a hard limit on the built sets, for both sides. Before the ladder, the solver settles every component of the order graph that holds a broken order ([`spreads/order-limit.ts`](./packages/replay-core/src/spreads/order-limit.ts)). The tiers run in order: a plausible spread (the usage camp) yields first; then the published body with its Speed IV at 31 (a published Speed IV below 31 is a Trick Room set's, and an order the log shows without Trick Room refutes it); then any legal Speed; then a Speed IV below 31; then a Choice Scarf on a first mover that may carry one. Inside a tier the Pokémon with its own Speed evidence keeps its Speed and the side without it absorbs the change, the second mover on a tie. A repaired order ends strict and a cycle tied; only a race that cannot end strict may tie. A weak race (a knock-out the victim may have lost to its own negative priority) binds while a legal set fits it. A component no tier settles keeps its sets. A race reads the forme that raced and a transformed mover's copied Speed.

**The ladder** ([`spreads/ladder.ts`](./packages/replay-core/src/spreads/ladder.ts)) crosses offense {0, max, max + nature} with bulk {uninvested, max HP, max HP + max Def or SpD, + nature} and Speed {kept, 0, max, max + nature}, plus the prior rung. Offense-plus and bulk-plus natures conflict, so those combinations are skipped. Every rung inherits the prior and overrides only what the observations can measure: offense with attacker observations, bulk with defender observations, Speed only under a move order. A prior nature that boosts a measured stat counts as Hardy for the rungs, so a rung that names no nature of its own carries no boost the observations are to measure. `keep` names prior stats the evidence cannot measure at all (an offense seen only in knock-outs, a Speed no rung can bring in line with the observed order): they keep the prior's value and give way last in the budget. `fixed` stats (the log's HP) override a rung's claim on them.

- A kept Speed keeps its plus nature, so the offense nature of the seen hit class (Adamant for physical lines, Modest for special) opens beside the kept Speed EVs; `bestRung` prices the order the neutral Speed may no longer win. A bulk nature never opens, and a kept offense keeps its nature.
- A kept Speed gives way to an offense claim the budget cannot express beside it, and then loses its plus nature. A bulk claim never releases Speed, and a kept offense never gives way. `bestRung` decides between the released body and the 0-offense rungs, and its order check prices a released body that no longer moves first.
- The leftover budget goes to the non-Speed stats. An unmeasured stat takes what fits; a measured stat takes the most EVs at which its damage lines fit as well as at the winning rung. Each stat first refills toward the prior's EVs, then the order offense, HP, Def, SpD and the other attack stat spends the rest. The log's HP stays.

## 2. The evaluation

### Search modes

- The MCTS endgame count (at most two living Pokémon a side) is the root's `MctsTreeStats.living`; a tree without the field keeps the floor.
- **Search budget.** Every search budget the app and the bank run on is one object (the trees, the iterations, the Auto split per game type, the team-preview lead). A measurement switch without a UI knob picks a form of it: `EVAL_SEARCH_BUDGET` under Node, the `ps-replay-interceptor:search-budget` key of `localStorage` in the browser, which the app stamps on every worker message. The syntax is a comma list of `key=value` over the default, read left to right: `trees`, `iterations`, `early-depth`, `early-samples` and `tree-from` (for both game types, or with a `singles-` or `doubles-` prefix for one), `lead-depth` and `lead-samples`. A typo or an out-of-range value throws.
- **Options a move fails by the body's own state.** `searchOptions` ([`search/options.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/search/options.ts)) starts from the simulator's legal choices, which let any body click Fake Out on its second turn or Sleep Talk awake. It then drops a move the simulator would fail by a fact the body already shows. A field move against a standing condition goes (a layered hazard at its cap), a sleep move goes under Sleep Clause while a foe sleeps, and `refusedByBody` ([`sim-ask/own-state.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/sim-ask/own-state.ts)) asks the move's own Try handler on a copy-on-write view of the battle. Two classes are asked: a handler that reads the body's action count (Fake Out, First Impression, Mat Block, refused from the body's first action on) and a move the Dex marks usable asleep (Sleep Talk, Snore, refused while the body is awake). The view raises the action count by one first, as `runMove` does before the Try event, and answers that an action is queued, which Mat Block's handler asks. Other Try handlers (Rest, Counter, Last Resort) read state that the turn itself changes, so they are never asked. In doubles the drop runs per slot before the hints rank the list, a slot whose every move is refused keeps them, and a list is never empty. A played move that failed this way finds no option and stays unmatched; so does a Sleep Talk played by a body that a faster foe put to sleep in the same turn, because the list reads the state at the start of the turn. The sweep `noop-moves-oracle.spec.ts` plays every Try-handler move of Generations 2 to 9 and the Champions mod, in singles and doubles, against the simulator's own verdict.
- **Progressive widening.** A tree node exposes only its strongest-by-hint unvisited options: the window opens at 4 options and gains one per 8 visits (`wideningWindow` in [`search/mcts-node.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/search/mcts-node.ts)). The hints are static, so the order is deterministic.
- **Hint prices.** The hints of a doubles node price two things beside the plain damage fraction. A self-boosting move earns the damage-fraction gain it would buy over two turns (`SETUP_HORIZON`), counting only the user's own stages that a damage fraction reads. A protector earns the most damage any known move of a foe would deal to the bodies it covers whose hit the protector's handler stops (the protector question in ARCHITECTURE-DETAILS.md), each body capped at its HP: a spread hit sums over the bodies, a single-target hit takes the larger one. A side protector covers the whole side (Crafty Shield denies no damage), and a Protect covers its user alone. The earned value is capped at 0.3 (`PAIR_KO_BONUS`); a Protect on a body that only two foes together can knock out earns the cap outright.

### How the search prices chance

- **Root quantile formula.** Tree k of T answers a named die with `U = (p(k) + u) / T`. `u` comes from a hash of the cell, the die and its occurrence and places the die inside its quarter; `p` is the die's own order of the T quarters (without it two 50 % dice never give "A yes, B no"). A binary die `randomChance(n, den)` comes out yes when `floor(U * den) < n`, `random(m)` answers `floor(U * m)`, `random(a, b)` answers `a + floor(U * (b - a))`, and a `sample` takes the index `floor(U * length)`. A bare `random()`, a shuffle (the speed tie) and an offset outside [0, T) stay on the stream. The generators draw the natural number first and replace it for a named die, so every unnamed draw and every fork below the root keeps the stream's number.
- **What a result keeps of a root cell** (`EvalMatrix.cells`): the tree visits pooled over the cell, whether the verify step re-priced it (a cell of one or two visits is a guess, and the hindsight line shows its number from 8 visits on, or when the cell is verified or a plain cell a tree expanded), and the planner's answer: `none` (a deterministic pair), `events` (a priced roll, with its outcome classes and their analytic weights, summing to 1) or `fail` (a guard tripped). The offset-0 tree ships the plan beside its boundary cells; a sibling tree's result, a matrix-mode result and a result cached before the field existed carry none. A singles root plans every cell. A doubles root ships the answer for the played row and column only, and only when the search was given the played turn (`keepPlayed`); the rest of its grid reads `fail`.
- **The pair plan's draws** ([`pair/`](./packages/eval-engine/src/pair/)). A recording generator writes down, per hit instance (one move object and one target, so a spread move is one instance per target), the accuracy roll with its threshold, the crit roll with its denominator, the damage roll with the crit flag and a snapshot of both bodies, each secondary die (one `random(100)` per entry the target's rules leave, Generation 3 and up) with its effect id, chance and result, and a speed tie among move actions. A move that doubles its strength on a chance (Fickle Beam) draws that chance before the damage roll, and the roll's snapshot carries the factor, so the table at the roll is the table of the strength the log shows. Only the move's own rolls are booked, so an attacker's status check and the confusion self-hit leave no record. A script answers one instance's rolls (keyed `${attackerSlot}:${moveId}>${targetSlot}`, a secondary by effect id); an answered roll still draws its number, so without scripts the draws are the plain generator's. A tree's root quantiles replace a named die's value after the natural number is drawn, the records carry the replaced value so that the battle's class reads true, and a script outranks a quantile. The singles solver keeps its own scripted generator (`forward/scripted-prng.ts`).
- **A pair cell is a weighted mix of outcome classes.** The first natural draw names the events; the heaviest outcome no draw showed yet is drawn next with the dice answering that one event the other way; each class weighs the product of its events' probabilities. The sampler stops at a coverage of 0.95, after eight draws (`PAIR_DRAW_BUDGET`), or when no candidate carries 0.01 of mass. A cell without events keeps the plain path, and a rule the plan does not price sends the cell to the plain mean over eight natural draws. The answer is `none` when the pair holds no accuracy roll and no kill range, else `events` with the classes heaviest first (ties by key), each key the sequence of the cell's events (`p1b:terastarstorm>p2b=hit-nokill`: attacker slot, move, target slot, outcome). A cell the plan hands to the plain mean, or cannot parse, is left out, which the matrix reads as failed.
- **Damage values.** The 16 damage values of a hit come from `@smogon/calc`, fed with the snapshot the dice took at the roll: a doubles field with the partner effects the calc knows (ruin, Helping Hand, Battery, Power Spot, Steely Spirit, Friend Guard, doubles screens). Three call details keep the calc on the simulator's number: `calculate` clones the move, so a spread move against one target goes in through `overrides`; the bodies come from the engine's one builder, which counts an entry boost once; and Meteor Beam and Electro Shot lose the charge boost the calc adds itself. The calc forces the spread target for Tera Starstorm of Terapagos-Stellar and for Expanding Force on Psychic Terrain, so its Doubles field takes the 0.75 spread factor off a hit that the simulator did not spread because only one foe stood; that hit is priced with the Singles game type. Such a hit that meets a screen is the one hit neither field prices (a screen is 2732/4096 in Doubles and 2048/4096 in Singles), so its table is null and the pair plan samples the simulator's own draws.

### The verify round and the answer round

**What a verified cell contributes** ([`mcts-merge.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/mcts-merge.ts)). The sampler prices the root chance split, and a tree's depth counts only where the pool has played the cell out and agrees. A cell without a class blend takes the pooled tree value when the pool is deep and the sampler's value otherwise. With a blend, every class is priced on its own: an ended class by its exact leaves, an open class by the pool of the trees that drew it when that pool is deep (from two trees), else by the sampler's class mean. Trees without a recorded class join a pool only when exactly one class is open. The verify step takes every open class one ply deeper and a plain cell's outcome groups mixed by share, so no verified cell mixes depths.

### Matchup, hazard and race terms

- **Race clocks.** Three coarse rules, fed by live simulator state, shape a pair's clocks. Heal PP absorb as survival: the remaining heal PP restore `healAbsorb` bars in total, so a defender soaks its HP plus that before it falls, and past the heal rate the held PP realize only at the ratio of heal rate to incoming damage. Action economy: a healer under pressure spends the share pressure over heal rate of its turns healing and attacks on the spare ones; at least its best heal rate it is pinned and priced as never attacking when it loses the pair either way, unless its attack KOs the other side no later than the other side KOs it, and then it attacks every turn. The PP budget caps every clock: a win that needs more turns than the attacker has PP never lands. Residuals alone can finish a race, but a side with no damaging move at all never wins one.
- **End of the turn.** The end-of-turn fractions (Leech Seed, Leftovers, Black Sludge, weather, terrain and the abilities of the weather) live in the simulator's code, not its data. So `fieldEvent('Residual')`, the call the simulator's own residual action makes, runs on a copy, and the HP of every active is read before and after over 3 turns (`RESIDUAL_TURNS`, the race horizon). The HP is set to 3/4 before each turn, so that neither the cap of a heal nor the cap of a hit bends the reading, and the durations of the weather, the terrain, the side conditions and the volatiles are set to lasting. The dice are fixed, so an effect that rolls (Shed Skin) is read on one seed. A reading is the loss per turn as a fraction of the body's own max HP, positive when the body loses HP, memoized under the board key, not under the battle. Only the actives have a reading, because the simulator ignores the item and the ability of a benched body (the races price a benched body's status damage from the status table), and Generations 1 and 2 have none (their status damage comes after the body's own move).
- **Hit costs.** Both sides pay for their hits. The HP a hit takes from its attacker (Rocky Helmet, Rough Skin, Iron Barbs, recoil, Life Orb) joins the attacker's residual, and the status it can leave joins as expected end-of-turn damage and expected lost damage. The simulator reads the hit by playing it ([`score/contact-play.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/score/contact-play.ts)): every draw of one played hit answers the same quantile `u` of its range (`random(n)` is `floor(u * n)`, a chance of n in d fires when `u < n / d`), and the hit is played on the grid `u = (k + 1/2) / 20`. Two probes (`u` = 0.075 and 0.925) come first: when the hit leaves the same status (or none) in both and the move does not roll its hit count, their mean is the cost; otherwise the other 18 points are played too. The play puts both sides at full HP, and the other bodies on the field too, because the chip is the amount of the effect and not what a body at low HP has left. A benched body is put on the field of the copy for the hit, as the static asks the simulator about benched bodies everywhere else. A recoil is read on a hit that does not run into the foe's last HP.
- **The price of a hit in the race** ([`score/contact-price.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/score/contact-price.ts)). The chip of a hit is paid right after the hit. The first mover has paid t chips when the other side lands its t-th hit and the second mover t - 1, so the clock is `ceil((HP + chip) / (hit + chip))`, and the chip of the hit that finishes the race decides nothing. With q the chance that some status lands on one hit, the first status stays, so it lands on hit j with chance `(1 - q)^(j - 1) * q`. It takes its chip at the end of the turns j to the turn before the other side's last hit, and only the hits after j deal its share of the damage. Paralysis, sleep and freeze take turns: the share of turns the body still moves on is kept turn by turn after the landing, and the halved Speed of a paralysis is not priced.
- **Boosts that bite.** A stage counts against a foe only when the side it helps wins the 1v1 race with that foe within the horizon (`BITE_TURNS`, three hits to a KO), gets to act in it, and the stage made the difference: it turned a lost race into a won one, or the win into a faster one. A drop is the mirror: the foe must win the race with it, get to act, and win by it. The pair's races come in three forms ([`score/boost-race.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/score/boost-race.ts)): `held` is the race on all the stages standing at the HP the bodies have and gates who wins, who acts and who closes the bench; `full` is the same race with both bodies at full HP, and `without` the race at full HP without one stat's stage. `full` against `without` says whether the stage decides a fresh race, because at the bodies' own HP the answer flips at single HP points. A stage does not bite a foe that plays Unaware, that can click a move that takes the stages away (`removesStages`, asked of the simulator), or that a wall holds anyway. While a foe on the field can take the stages away or KOs the holder before it acts, no foe counts for a boost. While a foe on the field wins the race within the horizon, or the holder does not win it against one, no foe off the field counts for any stage, unless a positive stage takes that foe's win away.
- **Status.** The flat multiplier of `bodies` prices a burn, poison or paralysis the same on every Pokémon, but some abilities (Poison Heal, Guts, Magic Guard) change what the status costs. So the multiplier is played on a twin battle ([`score/status-factor.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/score/status-factor.ts)). The twin reads six channels (HP over an idle turn, damage dealt and taken with a physical and a special move, speed) for the ability with and without the status, against a Pokémon with no ability. The status costs the channels where the plain statused Pokémon is worse than the plain healthy one. An ability that lifts one of its channels above its own healthy value turns the status into a gain (the multiplier is 1); otherwise the multiplier is the applied one plus the share of the cost the ability gives back. The idle turn is played on several seeds and the answer is the mean. Burn, paralysis, poison and Toxic are played; sleep and freeze keep the applied multiplier. `status-fix.spec.ts` holds every ability under every status to real battles.
- **The matchup memo is a function of its key**, because the app splits one matrix over a worker pool with one cache per worker. The key carries every read of `pairThreat` and `singleMoveFraction` (a new read needs its key term, held by `test/threat.spec.ts`): level, item, ability, choice lock, usable slots, current types, the Tera type, the stored stats it divides (Power Trick, Guard Split) and the defender's max HP. A move that halves the defender's HP (`HALVING_MOVES`) also keys the defender's current HP. A move whose answer at use reads a fact the key does not name (weather, terrain, grounding, stages, Hidden Power, a power the simulator sets from HP, status, field, speed or weight) keys its answer instead (`landedKey`, `CONTEXT_MOVES` in `move-use.ts`, the live class of `POWER_MOVES` in `move-facts.ts`). The usable-slot signature keys PP transitions.
- **The trend correction.** The tied leading rows take `corrected = deep + lambda * trend` ([`ranking/trend.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/ranking/trend.ts)), so a stall that bleeds under lookahead separates from a building switch by value. The shift moves a row's win chance (`ev`) and the order; its floor and its mean stay those of the published row, so the floor is the lowest cell of that row in the matrix dialog. The equilibrium is not re-solved, because re-solving lets the game absorb the correction; under fixed mixes the shift leaves the opponent's comparisons, the score, the mixes and the game value as they are. A group with any unpriced decisive cell forfeits whole (terminal cells price as trend 0). At depth 2 every live cell carries its own trend, so the layer asks for no probe there; a depth-1 root still probes the decisive cells of the tied rows, and restricted sub-searches skip the layer.

### The forced-win prover

- **The sure strike's queue question.** The foe's chances answer yes and the side's own chances answer no when the simulator's queue says whether the strike moves before each damaging foe move.
- **Flipped dice.** A die that a few draws all pass the same way (a second Protect that holds one time in three, a confusion's self-hit, an accuracy) would count as certain, so the prover plays each chance die of a turn the other way ([`dice/flip.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/dice/flip.ts)). The turn is recorded on a fork with the dice panel's recorder and played again with one die answered the other way and every other named die held to the recorded answer. The recorded turn is the plain turn of the same seed, and a fork never touches the battle it came from.
- **A worse class stands in.** A drawn class stands in for a class that no draw showed when it is at least as bad for the side: the same turn order and the same outcomes except one event, where the opponent's move missed or the side's own move knocked the target out (`dominates` in [`endgame/prover-order.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/endgame/prover-order.ts)).
- **A helping effect inherits.** A class in which a secondary effect fired inherits the proof of the class in which it did not, when the effect only helps the side that moved (`helpedSources` in [`endgame/prover-helping.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/endgame/prover-helping.ts)). The rule reads what the cell realized and never a name: the two class positions must differ by what the effect did and nothing else, the mover's stages higher and the foe's lower, with no Speed stage moved. A reaction (Defiant, Contrary, Mirror Armor, Eject Pack, a Clear Body that blocks the drop) shows as another difference, and those classes keep their own proofs. The fired class takes nothing over while a living body of the foe's side, the bench too, can click a move that reads or moves stages.
- **The simulator names the stage readers** (`foeReadsStages` in [`endgame/stage-readers.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/endgame/stage-readers.ts)), because the Dex has a field for some and none for the rest. The question plays the move in five runs, a Snorlax at a pure Water type clicking it first, with every die answering yes and the damage roll at its middle: a baseline with nobody staged, then the target with two stages gained in Attack and Special Attack, in Defense and Special Defense, and in Accuracy and Evasion, and the foe with two stages lost in all six. Speed is left out, because the helping rule never carries a class that moves it. A move reads the stages when, against the baseline, the target loses more HP, a target stage ends lower or a foe stage ends higher. A charge move plays its second turn.

## 3. Game analysis

### Turn verdicts

- **Sacrifice gates.** The gates read engine scores, and no score after the turn means no excuse, so game ends and gap turns fail closed. A body that dice denied gets no shape. Every own faint of the turn passes its own gate, so a plain loss or a body behind a stricter gate that fails blocks the leniency. The floor gate of a stayed feed lets the realized outcome exceed the played line's priced floor by at most 0.02 (`FEED_FLOOR_EPSILON`).
- **Read tolerance.** The paid-off credit tolerates measurement noise under the payoff margin (`RISK_PAYOFF_EPSILON`, 0.02). The tolerance widens praise and never excuses, so the feed payoff gate and the clearly-failed exit stay strict. A read on an untiered turn earns a card sentence only (`readCredit`): the played choice carried under 1 % of the equilibrium mix and was not the favourite, gave up some floor against the safe line, paid at once and grew a mistake-sized edge within the payoff window, and its side did not stand at a decided loss. It sets no `riskPaidOff`, so attribution, the report's read list and the totals stay unmoved.
- **Stopped odds.** A clause may quote a KO-odds claim ("Tera Starstorm kills ~4% of the time") that is priced on the board before the turn. When the opponent's reply stops the hit (Wide Guard turns Tera Starstorm away, a Protect a single hit), the clause drops the claim ([`turn-analysis/odds-stopped.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/turn-analysis/odds-stopped.ts), display only). The answer comes from the cell of the line against the click, which the search priced from the simulator's own turn, so no move name enters the rule. A cell the planner calls plain (`none`) holds no uncertain hit, so every claim of that cell never landed. A doubles cell lists the events of its classes with their attacker slot and move, and a claim without an event there never landed. A singles class names no move, so a singles cell that holds a roll keeps the claim, and so does a cell the search did not plan or failed on.

### The game report

- **The luck line.** The line says "luck" only while the visible dice do not dispute it. Where they run the other way by at least an inaccuracy (damage rolls leave no protocol marker, so a weaker anchor proves nothing), it keeps the number and says what it is, the ledger's residual: "Chance swings favored X overall (...), while the visible dice favored Y." With the ledger split into the rolls and the rest, the line says "luck" only when the rolls carry it; otherwise it names only what the rolls gave, to the side they favored, and under 1 % "The rolls favored neither side."
- **Key moments.** The top four stay (`REPORT_KEY_MOMENTS`), resolution turns stay excluded, and every other qualifying dice turn whose chance went against the winner joins them. A dice turn is a turn whose own rolls ran in its residual's direction or, without the turn's rolls, one that carries a protocol-visible dice event; without a winner or dice information no turn joins. The selection runs over the whole game, then a turn inside a standoff drops out unless it is a graded decision, and the slot of a dropped turn stays empty.
- **Standoffs.** Ten or more consecutive analyzed turns (`STANDOFF_MIN_TURNS`) at one fainted share in which nobody fainted and nobody switched ([`standoff.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/standoff.ts)). Singles read the raw played action and doubles the slot lists, because a label can carry a switch after another action, and a turn the analysis cannot see (no played tracking, no fainted share, a side that never acted) counts against the run. The report says "Turns 12 to 24 were a standoff: nothing fainted and nobody switched for 13 turns, so their shifts and reads stay out of the key moments." and keeps the paid-off reads of those turns off its read list.
- **Decided and forced claims, spoken once.** The report walk speaks a side's decided and forced sentences until the board leaves the decided zone. A claim spoken inside the zone is forgotten once a later turn's bar reads the side under `DECIDED_SCORE`, and a new sweep or proof speaks again. A proof spoken under the line (an open event holding the bar at 0.65) left no zone and stays spoken. Near keys stay: a near stage lives under the line by nature.

### Lines

- **A row's tail.** `rowLineTail` ([`src/lib/row-line.ts`](./src/lib/row-line.ts)) finds the engine line of the row in `EvalResult.lines` through `linesForMode`, by choice, by label or by a pivot move's name, and returns its steps after the first. A pivot row names the move and the body it brings in, so the replacement steps the line holds right after the move drop out. A row whose choice is `wait` returns no tail, whether or not the result holds lines; the choosing side's row carries the replacement. A result without `lines` returns `RankedChoice.line` as it is, again none for a waiting row.
- **The replacement in front of a matrix line.** A deepened cell's turn can end at a request that the matrix answers by the static pick. `resolveForcedSwitches` ([`forward/assignments.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/forward/assignments.ts)) returns one `StaticPick` for each round of its loop, labelled like a line step; the child position carries them as `staticPicks`, and the sub-search of the cell hands them on as `EvalResult.staticPicks` (sub-searches only; the field has no other reader). `recordDeepenedCell` writes the cell's line as the picks, the top pair, then the sub-search's own line, and `formatLine` reads a step flagged `replacement` as "replaces: ..." and joins the next step with a comma. A forced root searches its serialized child and carries no picks, so its cells keep their own line.
- **The end of a line.** A line stops where a side's request is `switch` after its last step, and `EvalLine.awaits` names that side or both. The line does not play the replacement, so its end value is the static value of the board before the switch-in; the Lines section says so in `endNote` ([`src/lib/line-view.ts`](./src/lib/line-view.ts)).
- **"(only choice)".** The lines job counts the choices the request leaves each slot of a side before it plays a step (`slotChoiceCounts` in [`forward/choice-counts.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/forward/choice-counts.ts)) and writes the slots with exactly one into `LineChoice.onlyChoice`. The count reads the options of `choiceList` ([`forward/choices.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/forward/choices.ts)) with Terastallization allowed whatever the search's setting, Mega Evolution included, and caps at two: 0 for a pass or a wait, 1, or 2 for more. A target on an ally and a Dynamax or Z-move that the list does not hold each count as a second choice, because the simulator accepts them. A slot whose moves are all barred, with no bench, counts 1, since the simulator turns the click into Struggle. The waiting side of a replacement step writes none. A sweep in `choices.spec.ts` plays every Dex entry that locks or traps a body or a move, in each generation and in Champions, singles and doubles, and compares the count with what the simulator accepts.
- **How the view words it.** A singles side, and a doubles side whose slots all hold one choice, read "X (only choice)". A doubles side with a free partner names the held slot: "X + Y (only choice: Protect)", or "→ Garchomp" for a switch, two held slots joined with "and". A replacement step reads "replaces: → Mew (only choice)" (`onlyChoiceNote` in `line-view.ts`).

### Win paths in a lost endgame

- **Step 1.** A position the dice reach that the proof did not (a roll a little off the proof's own draw) is proved again from there with the turns that are left.
- **Step 2, the dice search.** The search is best-first. A node is a step to play with some dice pinned, every other die at its likeliest result, and its priority is an upper bound of its path's chance: the product of the chances of its dice, with a damage roll and a speed tie counted as certain until the path wins (`exactChance` reads their share from the simulator on copies). Its successors are the next step and the same step with one more die turned the other way (a damage roll to the other side of its knock-out boundary, a speed tie to the other order), only dice after the last pinned one. The first win popped with its exact chance, once no bound in the queue beats it, is the likeliest. Dice no die kind names (a sleep duration, Outrage's lock) follow the seed. A damage roll counts its share only where it decides who falls: when the lowest and the highest roll leave the same bodies standing, the roll is no choice, and a body it leaves one hit from a later knock-out stays at the HP the path took, unpriced.

## 5. Measuring the engine

- **The dump and its readers.** `EVAL_CALIBRATION_DUMP` writes JSONL, one sample per line: `id`, `turn`, `tranche`, `phase`, `gameType`, `score`, `gameValue` (the root's solved matrix value, null without a full matrix), `faintedFraction`, `p1Won`, `decided` (the root's decided-sweep side or null), `decidedHeld` (that side where the finished score holds the sweep), `lastPair` (one living body per side), `rating`, `quality` (`hq` or `std`), `luckAgainstFavored`, `forcedWinMass`, `forcedWinTurns` and, with `EVAL_CALIBRATION_FEATURES=1`, `g` (the scaled feature vector). The harness prints the aggregate lines, `run-calibration.mjs` merges the slice dumps, and `paired-calibration.mjs` joins two dumps ([`scripts/calibration-lib.mjs`](./scripts/calibration-lib.mjs)). The math replicates `test/regression/fit-helpers.ts` (a pooled constant-K logistic fit by 500 iterations of gradient descent from 1.5, Brier under that K), and `compareSamples` is the summation order the harness sorts into before it aggregates, so a merged summary matches the single-process printout character for character.
- **Reusing a base.** `run-calibration.mjs` writes a fingerprint into `run.json`, and `--reuse` prints the folder of a finished run that carries the same one ([`scripts/calibration-fingerprint.mjs`](./scripts/calibration-fingerprint.mjs); the paired script's quick-tier header reads it too). `computeFingerprint` hashes the recipe's version, the Node version with platform and architecture, the git ids at HEAD of `FINGERPRINT_PATHS`, the sha256 of `node_modules/.package-lock.json`, the content of `.smogon-cache` and `.fit-corpus` (file count, bytes and one sha256 over every name, size and content) and the env (the run's env plus the ambient `EVAL_*`, `CALIB_*`, `NODE_OPTIONS` and `NODE_ENV` variables, without the two keys the runner sets per slice). The slice count is an input: `computeFingerprint` hashes it, so a run with another count never matches. A tree with an uncommitted change under the inputs gets a value that names HEAD; its run is marked dirty, never serves as a base, and a dirty tree never reuses (`decideReuse`). A run that adds entries to `.smogon-cache` records the cache it leaves behind, because the entries it fetched produced its rows. A run that lost replays to HTTP errors is incomplete, a change of any other input during the run marks it unstable, and a run without a fingerprint never matches. Everything under `test/regression/` is an input, so a ledger block booked into `eval-calibration.spec.ts` changes the value; a commit outside `FINGERPRINT_PATHS` keeps it.
