# PS Dashboard Evaluation Reference

This file says what the engine claims, what it guesses, and where it fails. Read it before you trust or doubt a number. The score is a heuristic estimate for spotting swings and blunders, not an oracle. [ARCHITECTURE.md](./ARCHITECTURE.md) covers how the code is layered; [README.md](./README.md) covers setup and commands.

## 1. What the app knows about a game

### Sources

The loader takes a Pokemon Showdown replay URL or id, including smogtours ids and private `-…pw` password links, and falls back to the replay server's `.log` route when the JSON route is unusable. A locally saved replay loads too: drop a "Download replay" `.html` file or a raw protocol log on the loader, or use `Browse file`. `?replay=<id|url>` loads a replay on startup and `?embed=1` hides the app chrome for an iframe host (README, "Embedding").

### Revealed, sheet, guessed, manual

Every field of both teams carries its source, and the stats panel shows it:

- revealed: proven by the protocol (moves, items, abilities, levels, gender, Tera type), including ability reveals from effect attributions, item reveals from heal messages, mega stones, Life Orb recoil and Rocky Helmet chip,
- sheet: from an open team sheet (`|showteam|` or a "View team" chat export), authoritative for items, abilities, moves and EVs the protocol never showed,
- guessed: usage statistics and set assumptions; a spread fitted to observed damage shows as "fitted", a Choice Scarf the move order proved as "inferred", an item the log's silence points to (Heavy-Duty Boots for a switch-in that took no Stealth Rock damage, Protective Pads for an attacker whose contact hit a shown Rocky Helmet did not chip) with that reason,
- manual: your edits and imports.

Revealed and manual knowledge outrank the sheet, and the sheet outranks guesses. The simulator teams for branching and evaluation follow the same order, so a usage guess never overwrites a sheet item. Placeholders stand in for unrevealed Random Battle slots.

### Rule-outs

The protocol disproves guesses, and a guess never contradicts the replay:

- hazard, status, weather or Life Orb damage rules out Magic Guard,
- Stealth Rock chip rules out Heavy-Duty Boots,
- a landed move hit rules out the abilities that would have refused it: a `-damage` without `[from]` inside a move's action. The twin battle that reads a landed effect plays the hit through the simulator's own turn, under the field and with the target's item, types and conditions the log shows, once for each ability the target may hold and each ability the attacker's species may hold. An ability counts as refused only when it refuses the hit in every case of the attacker while the scene with no ability lets it land. The Dex decides which abilities refuse (Water Absorb, Storm Drain, Volt Absorb, Flash Fire, Sap Sipper, Soundproof, Bulletproof, Wonder Guard and Levitate are some), and in doubles the same hit rules out the ally's redirect (Lightning Rod, Storm Drain). A move that sets its own type (Weather Ball, Tera Blast, Judgment, Hidden Power: an `onModifyType` handler), a Z-move, a Max move, a move used under Electrify or Ion Deluge, an attacker that may ignore the guard (Mold Breaker kin), a target that transforms in the twin (Imposter copies the foe on entry, so the scene is another battle), a party that may hold an Illusion user and a refusing set that is the species' whole set give no verdict; the reader counts the skips. The party counts as such when the team preview shows an Illusion-capable species, and in a format with no team preview (Random Battle) from Generation 5 on: a disguised Zoroark takes the hit under the name and species of its disguise, so the verdict would land on the wrong species. Only the candidates that could refuse a hit at all are asked: an ability with a handler in a hit's steps (`TryHit`, `TryMove`, `RedirectTarget`, `Damage` and their ally, foe and any twins), one that grounds the body, starts a field when it enters or changes the body's types (found by asking the simulator on a bare twin, once per ability), so a body whose candidates hold none costs no scene, and the sweep holds that list to the Dex. The log shows Gravity, Ingrain and Smack Down but not a hidden Iron Ball, so a Levitate holder under one reads as no Levitate holder (the Ground rule it replaces had the same hole),
- two distinct plain moves without switching rule out every Choice item (Dancer species excepted; called moves like Sleep Talk never count),
- a plain status move rules out Assault Vest,
- a Reflect, Light Screen or Aurora Veil that ends in the residual phase after the plain five turns rules out Light Clay for its setter, and one that ends after eight turns proves it. The turns come from the Dex condition (`conditionTurns` in [`dex-facts/screen-turns.ts`](./packages/replay-core/src/dex-facts/screen-turns.ts)); the log reader is [`inference/screen-evidence.ts`](./packages/replay-core/src/inference/screen-evidence.ts). A screen that an action removes (Brick Break, Defog), that a move such as Court Change moves to the other side, that still stands at the log's end, or that starts with `[from]` or `[silent]` gives no verdict. Neither does a screen whose setter showed an item before, swapped it, may be an Illusion user, may have Klutz, or stood under Embargo or Magic Room. Generations 1 to 3 have no Light Clay and give none.
- a logged effect rules out the ability that would have refused it: a flinch, an unboost, a status or a volatile that lands on a body rules out each ability of its candidates that stops that effect from that source, and in doubles the partner's guard (Flower Veil, Sweet Veil) too. The reader asks the simulator on a twin battle of the game's generation, mod and game type ([`sim-ask/effect-refusal.ts`](./packages/replay-core/src/sim-ask/effect-refusal.ts), read by [`inference/blocked-effects.ts`](./packages/replay-core/src/inference/blocked-effects.ts)), so no ability list stands in the code; the landed-hit rule above asks the same question of a move's hit. The refusing handlers are the Dex's (`onTryAddVolatile`, `onTryBoost`, `onSetStatus`, `onImmunity`, `onModifySecondaries`, `onTryHit`, and the `onAllyTry*` and `onAllySetStatus` twins that guard the partner). The twin runs the events the real move runs: the move's own `ModifyMove` event (a Mold Breaker kin sets `ignoreAbility` there, and the battle's own `suppressingAbility` then skips the breakable handlers), the secondary filter for an effect that a move's secondary adds, `TryHit` for a status move, then the add or boost call. The log names the effect's source: the Intimidate-like ability line just before a drop (the announced ability counts, whatever copied it: Transform, Trace), the move resolving now (a status move's own effect, or a secondary of a damaging move, read from the Dex move's data) or, for a flinch, a move that hit the body this turn. Each candidate is asked for every source the line could name and every ability the source's species could hold, and a scene the log contradicts proves nothing. A line with an ability or item source and a source that may ignore abilities (Mold Breaker) give no verdict. Neither does a log that names Neutralizing Gas, a party that may hold an Illusion user, a body that is transformed, in another forme than its team entry, or has its ability suppressed or changed, or a refusing set that is the whole set the species has left: that is a misread or a rule the simulator lacks, never a proof, and `blockedContradictions()` counts it (`blockedSkips()` counts the effects the reader could not judge at all).
- a weather or terrain that ends after its plain turns rules out the item that would have stretched it, and one that ends after that item's turns proves it. Sunny Day lasts five turns, eight with Heat Rock; Rain Dance runs longer with Damp Rock, Sandstorm with Smooth Rock, Hail and Snowscape with Icy Rock, and the four terrains with Terrain Extender (`conditionTurns` reads every pair from the Dex condition; the log reader is [`inference/field-evidence.ts`](./packages/replay-core/src/inference/field-evidence.ts), the sibling of the screen reader, with its verdict and its doubts). The lifetime counts the residual phases the field stood: a start in the action phase counts the turn it started on, a start before the first turn (a lead's ability) or after the turn's `|upkeep` (a replacement's ability) counts from the next turn. The setter is the user of the move line before the start when the Dex move sets that condition, or the `[of]` body of a `[from] ability:` start; an ability's start counts only where it does not leave the field standing for good (before Generation 6 an ability's field never ends). The turns prove nothing, and no verdict follows, where the field ends outside the residual phase (a removal), a second weather or terrain replaces the first outside the residual phase (a replaced terrain prints only the new start), the field still stands at the log's end, the start falls in the residual phase, or the start carries `[from]` of a move (Metronome, Copycat) or names no setter.
- an item whose trigger the log shows without the item's line is ruled out. An action or an entry opens a window, the next action boundary closes it, and an item whose own handler would have fired shows no line. [`inference/trigger-evidence.ts`](./packages/replay-core/src/inference/trigger-evidence.ts) reads Air Balloon, Booster Energy and Eject Pack; [`inference/item-triggers.ts`](./packages/replay-core/src/inference/item-triggers.ts) reads Lum Berry (a status or a confusion), Sitrus Berry (half HP, unless Heal Block stops the heal), Eject Button (a damaging hit of another Pokémon while a teammate can come in) and Red Card (the hit's attacker leaves while its side can switch); [`inference/field-item-triggers.ts`](./packages/replay-core/src/inference/field-item-triggers.ts) reads the four terrain seeds (Generation 7 on) and Room Service (Generation 8 on): a holder that enters while its terrain or Trick Room stands, a terrain that starts with the holder on the field, a pseudo-weather that starts while Trick Room stands (a second Trick Room that ends the first runs no event). Each condition comes from running the item's own handler on a stand-in holder over the field the log shows, so the half-HP line, the status test and the switch guards are the simulator's, and no table of ours says which terrain arms which item. The gates are the simulator's too: a Pokémon ignores its item under Embargo, Magic Room or Klutz; a foe with Unnerve or As One stops a berry; Sheer Force removes a move's secondary effects and with them Eject Button and Red Card; one spread move sends only the first Eject Button or Red Card away. The seeds and Room Service read their gates at the trigger: Magic Room and Embargo stop every item at that moment, and a block that ends later does not release the item (the next trigger decides). A Pokémon that may have Klutz is not judged (Neutralizing Gas turns Klutz off, so the rule stays out of that case on purpose), and +6, Contrary and Commander do not stop a seed. [`inference/stat-items.ts`](./packages/replay-core/src/inference/stat-items.ts) holds the three stat items' census and [`inference/stat-item-triggers.ts`](./packages/replay-core/src/inference/stat-item-triggers.ts) the stages it reads from the log's boost lines; Magic Room, Embargo and a possible Klutz stop all three, and a Baton Pass, a swap, a Psych Up, a blocked Intimidate or a Substitute that takes it gives no verdict. Only items still unknown and never swapped are ruled out, and a team that may hold an Illusion user is not judged.
- a move's last PP spent with no berry line in the log rules out a Leppa Berry: a Toxic used to its maximum, Pressure's extra PP, a Revival Blessing used and offered again. `ppEaters` ([`inference/item-triggers.ts`](./packages/replay-core/src/inference/item-triggers.ts)) lists the items of the Dex that eat at 0 PP, and Klutz, Embargo, Magic Room, Unnerve, an Illusion team and a known or swapped item give no verdict.
- an item that its holder's own move or a hit on its holder spends, and that printed no line, is ruled out. Throat Spray goes after a damaging sound move of its holder that hit another Pokémon. Weakness Policy and Enigma Berry go after a super-effective hit, Kee Berry and Jaboca Berry after a physical hit, Maranga Berry and Rowap Berry after a special one, and Absorb Bulb, Luminous Moss, Cell Battery and Snowball after a hit of their type, each from the generation that has the item. White Herb goes when a stat of its holder stood below 0 at the next action boundary (a switch, a move or the end of the turn) and no item line followed, Mirror Herb when a foe raised a stat while the holder stood on the field, and Adrenaline Orb when an Intimidate lowered the holder's Attack (or raised it through Contrary) while its Speed stood below +6. [`inference/spent-items.ts`](./packages/replay-core/src/inference/spent-items.ts) runs each item's own handler on a stand-in holder with the logged move and the effectiveness the log printed, and its sweep holds the list to the Dex. The gates are the simulator's: Klutz, Embargo and Magic Room stop the item; Unnerve and As One stop a berry; Heal Block stops Enigma Berry; an attacker that may have Magic Guard stops Jaboca Berry and Rowap Berry; Sheer Force on a move with secondaries stops Throat Spray, Kee Berry and Maranga Berry and leaves Weakness Policy alone; a Substitute that takes the hit leaves the holder no damage line. A holder that faints in the hit, a status sound move, and a move whose type or category the move or the attacker's ability may change (Shell Side Arm, Tera Blast, Normalize, Liquid Voice, Electrify, Ion Deluge) give no verdict for an item that reads them. Only items still unknown and never swapped are ruled out, and a team that may hold an Illusion user is not judged.
- a contact hit into a Rocky Helmet that the log already showed, with no chip on the attacker, tells Protective Pads for an attacker whose item the log has not shown, and the build plays the Pads. This is a guess, not a rule-out. The Dex decides which items take contact away: `contactLands` in [`inference/contact-tell.ts`](./packages/replay-core/src/inference/contact-tell.ts) asks the simulator's `checkMoveMakesContact` after the item's own `onModifyMove`, and the tell needs exactly one legal item that does so for the move. A punch in Generation 9, which Punching Glove also stops, tells nothing, and neither does any move in Pokémon Champions, where the Pads are not legal. An attacker that may have Long Reach or Magic Guard, a holder that may have Klutz or stands under Embargo or Magic Room, a holder that faints in the hit, a party that may hold an Illusion user and an attacker whose item swapped give no tell. The tell is written after the last line, because an item value set earlier would stop every later rule-out of that Pokémon, and the Heavy-Duty Boots tell goes through the same writer. Neither is written when a line named the item, the log ruled it out, or the format's Item Clause is already used up by the items the side's Pokémon showed and the tells already written.

A rule-out moves the guess to the next candidate instead of dropping to nothing.

### Usage and set guesses

Usage stats come from `data.pkmn.cc`, the only host that sends CORS headers, with the GitHub Pages mirror behind it when the primary host fails outright; a 404 counts as an answer. `@pkmn/smogon` supplies set assumptions for abilities, items, moves, natures and EVs. Guesses merge per species along a fallback chain: the format's own stats file, then the Smogon doubles ladder for VGC, the generation's OU, and Ubers. VGC reads the file of its year; Pokémon Champions VGC reads its own (`gen9championsvgc2026`), since Champions plays with megas, its own items and its own stat scale. A species missing from a file still gets guessed moves, items and abilities. Set assumptions come from the format's set file, then the generation's Ubers file (Doubles Ubers for doubles); VGC reads the set file of its year and never the Doubles OU analyses. Each guess names the file it came from. An ability no file can guess defaults to the species' first ability that the log did not rule out and the format allows, in the order slot 0, slot 1, hidden ability. The format's own rules decide what it allows: its validator bans Sand Veil and Snow Cloak in the OU tiers, Arena Trap and Moody in Gen 9 OU, Commander in Doubles OU. A tier the simulator does not know, such as a draft league, reads its bans from the `|rule|` lines of its log, and a Custom Game without such lines bans nothing. A species whose every ability is out keeps slot 0. When the set files cannot load at all, the top bar says "Smogon sets unavailable" instead of guessing without them.

A move the log shows books to the body that used it: a Pledge combination or Round books the shown move and not the partner's, and a body under Transform books its moves to the body it copied (`bookingIdent` in [`inference/client-facts.ts`](./packages/replay-core/src/inference/client-facts.ts)), so the copied body holds the moves its Ditto used. A corpus sweep holds every logged own move to the built set on every build path that has no manual edit; a pasted or edited set can lack a logged move, because a manual edit beats the sheet and the log.

A guessed set assembles as one unit. Published sets are scored against the revealed evidence, the winner fills the unrevealed slots, and pairwise vetoes trim the marginal fills: a big attack the set's boost does not serve falls (a Swords Dance Cobalion never guesses Body Press), an orphaned defense boost falls with its payoff, same-type redundancy collapses, and a Choice or Assault Vest guess suppresses guessed status moves. The stats panel and the simulator run the same guesser, and "Analyze game" waits for the Smogon fetches.

Details: [EVALUATION-DETAILS.md](./EVALUATION-DETAILS.md#usage-and-set-guesses).

### Fitted spreads and inferred Scarf

Hidden EV spreads are fitted to the damage the replay showed. Every clean hit, in singles and doubles, becomes a damage observation; a deterministic solver checks standard spread candidates against `@smogon/calc` roll ranges. It replaces a usage-guessed spread only where at least two observations demand it, never touches revealed or edited spreads, and reads offense only from attacking observations and bulk only from defending ones. A body whose EVs the user set by hand plays the nature the panel shows beside them: the solve still answers for it, but a nature fitted to the solver's own EVs never joins the user's EVs, so the panel and the battle read one spread ([`team/set-resolvers.ts`](./packages/replay-core/src/team/set-resolvers.ts), `shownNature`; issue 150: a panel that showed Jolly played Hardy and lost a Speed tie the game never had). Speed comes from the replay's own races: same-turn move order and knock-outs landed before the victim acted become speed constraints. The parser drops any order something other than Speed explains (priority, Prankster, Quick Claw, Trick Room turns, weather speed abilities, copied actions). A knockout reads by the game's display bounds: the log shows the HP as a percentage that Scarlet/Violet rounds up and Pokémon Champions rounds down, as a 48-pixel bar, or exactly in a Custom Game ([`hp-display.ts`](./packages/replay-core/src/hp-display.ts)), so the least damage of a knockout is the lowest HP the shown text allows less the highest HP left, and no slack stands in for it. A knockout that no spread of the ladder reaches, with a guessed item, lets the solver try the usage-listed boost items in usage order and take the first whose fit reaches every knockout of the attacker with a lower total error ([`spreads/item-rung.ts`](./packages/replay-core/src/spreads/item-rung.ts)); the Item Clause and the teammates' known items bound the offers. Candidates respect the format's EV budget (508 total and 252 per stat; Pokémon Champions uses 66 Stat Points and 32 per stat).

A solve that misfits its own evidence falls back to the usage prior. Solved spreads reach every simulator team.

What the fit cannot prove: "the defender is bulkier" and "the attacker is weaker" fit the same damage line, so the solve guarantees consistency with the replay, not the true spread. A knock-out line is only a lower bound, so an attacker seen only in knock-outs keeps its guessed offense. A move order no legal spread reproduces keeps the guessed Speed.

A move order the mover cannot reach at full Speed reads as a Choice Scarf. The solver measures the mover against the slowest plausible opponent spread (common usage spreads, or the real one when a sheet or edit fixed it) and gives the Scarf only when even that opponent stays out of reach, the Scarf closes the gap, and the species runs Speed-invested spreads at all. The rule also runs backwards: a guessed Scarf that would outrun an observed order is dropped and the build takes the next item. Known items, sheet sets and protocol rule-outs stay untouched, and the sensitivity probes skip an inferred Scarf.

Details: [EVALUATION-DETAILS.md](./EVALUATION-DETAILS.md#fitted-spreads-and-inferred-scarf).

### Editing and importing sets

Edits for either player (`Edit Player` and `Edit Opp` in the top bar's `Setup` menu) rebuild the branch and replay its history. The editor offers legal pools: the species' own moves, generation-legal items, the species' abilities, Tera types and natures. A species the generation's standard Dex hides (a Past species such as Dracovish or Zygarde in a National Dex draft) takes its pool and its abilities from the battle's own rules: the simulator's pool for it (`species.getMovePool`, National Dex when the format plays Past species) and its Dex entry; a format id the simulator does not list plays as the Custom Game of its generation, and a standard format keeps refusing such a species. The dropdown lists the generation's standard moves beyond the species' own, and a typed name takes every move the battle plays, marked "not learnable" outside the species' pool. `Import/Export Sets` round-trips both teams as Showdown text (natures, IVs, levels included); imports persist per replay as manual knowledge, and so does an edit saved in the editor: `Save` writes both edited sides as they stand (an import's values included) to one versioned record per replay, which replaces the stored import text, and a reload restores the record before any stored import text applies on top of it (section 6). An import that names a move, ability, item, nature or Tera type the editor would not offer, or a species that is not on the team, imports nothing and lists what to fix. Hypothetical moves ("What if it had …") load into the set.

## 2. The evaluation

### Search modes

A sim-backed search plays every legal choice pair on forked battles and solves the choice matrix as a zero-sum game by regret matching. A move the simulator would let fail for a fact the body already shows is no option: a first-turn move after the body's first action, a sleep-only move on an awake body, a field move against the condition that stands. Sampling is deterministic with fixed seeds and KO-boundary roll grouping, and the work fans out over a worker pool sized by the machine's cores, so pool size moves only wall-clock. The engine selector offers four modes:

- depth 1 and depth 2: the full joint matrix, with deeper cells valued by a shallower sub-search. At an open replacement request (a knock-out, U-turn, Volt Switch, Flip Turn) the matrix reads each switch-in by the turn that follows it (round 89). Every cell's board gets the engine's own search of that turn (depth 1, the root's draws, no prover), and the cell books that turn's solved value in place of the static of the board on arrival; the turn search is the cell's horizon, so the trend layers add no shift to it. A tie among the top rows then moves by the one-ply trend on the win chance and the order only; a row's floor stays its lowest cell. The cells go in together or not at all: a stop leaves the arrival values, so no row mixes the two scales. The app stops a search by terminating its workers (ADR 0011) and keeps the last partial result of that position as the answer: the dock fills its chips from it and Execute stays, and the panel reads "Search stopped. Showing the quick estimate." (after a later level: "Showing the result so far."). The tree streams its first interim result after 150 iterations of its first tree (a third to a half of a tree's time, about 4 seconds in a Node probe), and a stop after it keeps that result like any other partial (it reads "Showing the result so far.", at the tree's own depth, not the quick estimate). A stop before it (a tree before its 150th iteration, or one still loading in the worker) leaves no suggestion, and the panel says "Search stopped before a first result." beside Evaluate. The result reads depth 2 (the panel says "depth 2"); an explicit depth 2 adds nothing at such a root, and depth 3 deepens on from there. The arrival static let bodies tie, and one that lost its race a turn later could lead: after U-turn the engine sent Glimmora in against a Choice Scarf Gholdengo that takes it in two turns, where Great Tusk holds. The price is one turn search per switch-in. On three probes (the shared game and two corpus replacements, five bodies each) the replacement took 24 to 75 seconds on the dev machine, which runs 2.5 to 3 times slower than a normal one, against 0.4 to 0.7 seconds before. The tree needs none of this: Auto uses it in doubles and in singles from a quarter fainted, and its forced node is a decision node that plays the next turn. Inside a turn, the matrix's cells, the verify sampler, play-outs and the prover still resolve a replacement by the static pick (a deepened cell's line names that pick as its own step, so a matrix line reads a knock-out or a pivot as a tree line does: "then replaces: → Dragonite, Power Gem · Dragon Dance").
- MCTS: a DUCT tree search (four trees of 600 iterations, each expansion drawing its dice from a seed hashed from the tree and the iteration; see "How the search prices chance"). The app asks the first tree for its interim result every 150 iterations until a whole tree has ended (the ranking, score and line of the tree so far, no change to the search), so the panel shows a result in seconds and a stop keeps it. A forced switch after a knock-out is a decision node inside the tree; the verify sampler, play-outs and prover resolve replacements by the static pick. Chance-suspect root cells are re-verified by the matrix sampler, so a lucky miss in one tree cannot masquerade as a good line. A cell the trees played out over enough visits, with the trees in agreement, keeps the trees' value when it reads a decided game (±0.9). In an endgame of at most two living Pokémon a side it keeps any value once the trees visited the cell 100 times: there the trees play the game to its end, and the sampler's single ply turned the ranked lines against the score; a thinner pool stays with the sampler, which can see the win a short line hides. When the trees disagree there (the spread tips the 0.075 bar), the pool keeps its value only if the sampler's value lies outside the range of the means of the trees that visited the cell 8 times or more and the sampler does not read a decided game (±0.9): then no tree that played the cell sides with the one-ply read. A pool whose tree means bracket the sampler stays with it.
- Where the user picks the mode: the `Depth` select in the top bar's `Setup` menu (1, 2, MCTS or Auto), with `Samples` (1, 3 or 5) beneath it while a matrix depth is chosen; the Evaluation panel's header holds neither since release 1.2.0. The same menu holds `Tera` (shown where the format's simulator rules offer Tera, not in Champions, under Terastal Clause or before Generation 9; shown with any engine, since the engine request carries it) and, above `Depth`, the `Engine` select once a second engine exists; another engine hides `Depth` and `Samples` only. The rows lock while a search runs. The choice is stored in the browser (`mode`, `depth`, `samples`), so a stored matrix depth stays in force and Auto is one pick away.
- Auto, the default: singles run a depth-1 matrix with three samples while boards are full and the tree once a quarter of all Pokémon have fainted; doubles run the tree from the first turn. The team-preview lead stays on a depth-1 matrix in both game types.

Pivot moves (U-turn, Volt Switch, Flip Turn, Parting Shot, Baton Pass and the like) are move-plus-incoming pairs over the live bench, so the engine can say which Pokémon makes a pivot safe. Guaranteed-failing clicks are dropped from the candidates.

Tera enumeration is a setting, the `Tera` row of the Setup menu. Auto follows the format in a new game (Tera is offered wherever the simulator's rule table and the mod allow it; a Terastal Clause format, a generation before 9 and Champions stay off) and infers "banned" from a replay that never terastallized; in a draft or Custom Game, new or replayed, Auto and Revealed restrict Tera to the Pokémon that terastallized in the log, so a new draft game starts with none.

Doubles search combined two-slot choices (per-slot targets, spread moves, one Tera, Mega Evolution or Ultra Burst per turn) over a candidate list cut down per side. Static threat hints rank the combos, distinct move cores fill the budget before gimmick variants, and the pair played in the real game always stays so its regret remains computable. A turn with a hidden slot action (a flinch, a fainted partner), the grade reads the visible slot alone, except a slot that fell before it acted, which it grades as the best row inside the order bound (see Stay-in).

### How the search prices chance

An accuracy check, a kill roll and a secondary effect decide what a cell is worth, so the search names them and weighs them by the Dex chance. It does not trust how often five fixed seeds happen to hit.

- Root cells: a cell whose outcome hangs on one of those dice is priced as a mix of outcome classes. A class reads like `hit-nokill`, `hit-nokill+brn` (the burn came true), `miss` or `hit-kill`. Accuracy and kill odds come from `@smogon/calc` ([`ko-odds.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/ko-odds.ts)). The chance of a secondary comes from the simulator through the dice module ([`dice/secondaries.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/dice/secondaries.ts)), after the user's and the target's rules (Serene Grace, Sheer Force, Shield Dust, a King's Rock flinch). The search draws a class the seeds never reach by script, one draw per missing class (`forceMissingClasses` in [`search/cell-sampler.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/search/cell-sampler.ts), `forceMissing` in [`search/outcome-children.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/search/outcome-children.ts)); an effect under 1 % of the mass stays unpriced (`MIN_CLASS_MASS`). A flinch cuts the second mover inside its class, read from the Dex as a one-turn condition with a `BeforeMove` handler.
- Root quantiles: at a root cell tree k of four answers each named die with a quantile of its own ([`search/root-quantile.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/search/root-quantile.ts)). A die alone gets one tree in each quarter of its range, so a die of chance p comes true in floor(4p) or ceil(4p) of the four trees and a die of a quarter or more is never missed by all four; the order of the quarters differs from die to die, so two dice stay independent across the trees. The merge weighs each class by its Dex chance (`keyedCell` in [`search/merge-pool.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/search/merge-pool.ts)).
- Doubles trees: the root draw of each tree names its class through the pair plan ([`pair/tree-key.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/pair/tree-key.ts), `doublesRootChild` in [`search/expansion.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/search/expansion.ts)). The merge ([`mcts-merge.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/mcts-merge.ts)) prices each class with the trees that drew it and with its Dex weight. Singles trees book the same kind of key for their root cells.
- Below the root the tree stays flat: it holds no chance nodes (ADR 0023, which supersedes ADR 0009). The seed of an expansion is `treeSeed(iteration, seedOffset)` in [`search/leaf.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/search/leaf.ts), a hash of the tree's number and the iteration. The five fixed seeds the search used before (`SEARCH_SEEDS`, now kept in `packages/eval-engine/test/seed-streams.ts` for the specs that name the old streams) are one arithmetic progression of the simulator's generator. Under them, at one draw index a 10 % effect came true in none or in a fifth of the five seeds, and in the singles tree one seed carried it in 74 % of its cells while the other four carried it in at most 2 %; a 90 % move missed in 73 % of the second seed's cells. With hashed seeds the pooled rate of each band with 150 or more events sits within 1.5 points of the Dex chance in the singles tree, Doubles OU and Champions VGC, and in VGC except accuracy 95 to 99 % (91.0 against 95.0 on 156 events), and two cells no longer share an outcome. Every other path reads the seeds of its own cell: the matrix, the verify round, the pair search, the trend probe and the endgame children take slot s of the cell's list (`cellSeed(s, key)` in [`search/leaf.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/search/leaf.ts), `key` the hash of the position and the two choices), so two cells never share one dice stream and the paths that read one cell share its list. On the old fixed list a 20 % secondary came true in 73 to 80 % of a singles matrix and a critical hit at 1.4 % against 4.2 %.
- The scripted dice that ask for a class name each draw by the simulator's own hook through `DrawNamer` ([`dice/namer.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/dice/namer.ts), ADR 0002). A paralysis, infatuation or confusion check is no longer read as the move's own accuracy or crit.

### The verify round and the answer round

A tree fixes one chance outcome per root cell when it creates the cell, and every later visit descends through that same child. A cell's visit count therefore measures how deep its subtree went, not how often its dice were drawn: one sample per tree. A tree that rode a missed 90 % move through its whole subtree reads the cell far from the three trees beside it. After the merge, the verify round re-prices the cells the trees cannot vouch for.

- Which cells ([`verify-select.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/verify-select.ts)). The support of each side is every option with at least 5 % of the equilibrium mass, plus the ranked top three and the played option at a nominal mass, so a row the solve demoted on noise still verifies. A support cell is chance-suspect when the pool holds fewer than 8 visits of it (`VERIFY_MIN_VISITS`), when fewer than 3 trees expanded it (`VERIFY_MIN_TREES`; fewer when the search ran fewer trees), or when the trees that did expand it disagree: the visit-weighted mean absolute deviation of the per-tree means from the pool mean passes 0.075 (`VERIFY_DISAGREEMENT`). Two equal camps more than 0.15 apart tip a cell (exactly 0.15 reads 0.075 and does not); a thin outlier beside three deep trees does not. The boundary cells of the doubles pair plan are suspect whatever their visits, and a search runs at most 12 cell jobs (`VERIFY_CELL_CAP`).
- How a cell is priced ([`search/verify-cell.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/search/verify-cell.ts)). The cell goes one ply deeper than the trees read it. A cell with a class plan (the singles class plan, the doubles pair plan) goes one ply deeper per open class through the class's own first draw and keeps the plan's weights. A cell without a plan draws 16 natural seeds (`VERIFY_PLAIN_DRAWS`), groups them by outcome (who fell, who missed, who could not act) and goes one ply deeper per group, mixed by draw share. An outcome goes deeper through one child per state its draws leave, mixed by that state's share, because one child cannot stand for what the log does not split: a Sleep Powder hit whose sleep counter reads 3 in one child, while a third of its draws wake next turn, prices far from the average over all of them. Singles split on every state the simulator keeps on a Pokémon (HP apart); doubles split only on the effects that keep a count (a sleep, confusion, trap, lock, Protect or Encore counter, Toxic's stage), since the full split costs about 40 % more sub-searches for the same verdicts (`VERIFY_STATE_RULE`).
- The cover. Outcomes go deeper by descending weight until a share of the cell's open weight is covered (90 % in singles); the rarer rest takes its one-ply value shifted by the deepened outcomes' weighted mean step, so no cell mixes depths. A doubles sub-search costs about three singles ones, so doubles goes deeper through the heaviest outcome only (`VERIFY_DEEPEN_COVER`) and prices the rest beyond it by a light sub-search (`VERIFY_CHEAP`, `VERIFY_LIGHT`): a 5 x 5 matrix, a fifth of a full sub-search's time, and an outcome lighter than 5 % keeps the shift.

A doubles root lists 16 combinations per side (the hint cut of `search/options.ts`), so its best row can read 1.00 against every listed answer while an unlisted legal answer refutes it (VGC 2630685175 turn 7: Wood Hammer with Earth Power against the 12 listed answers, each of Zamazenta's six Protect answers holds it near 0.75). The answer round ([`search/answers.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/search/answers.ts)) grades each side against the other side's legal answers, taken from the simulator's legal list and never from a hand list.

- Detection. In the default form (`protect`; `EVAL_DOUBLES_ANSWERS` switches it under Node: `full` or `off`) the side's best row meets every legal answer with one natural draw, and an unlisted answer is added when it beats the listed ones by more than 0.05 against that row (`ANSWER_MARGIN`), at most 4 per side (`ANSWER_CAP`). Which of several rows within a hair of the top stands first is a tree's noise, so detection reads the best row and its near ties (up to 3 rows within `PROVER_NEAR_TIE`): the best row against every answer, the others against the listed answers and the 8 unlisted answers that read lowest against the best row, and each candidate meets the highest near row it refutes. `full` adds every unlisted legal answer.
- Confirmation. A candidate counts only when it still refutes the row one ply deeper (a depth-1 search of its child, the depth the verify round prices a cell at). At one ply a stall answer reads the board's static, which can lie far from the trees' value: a double Protect read -0.16 for the foe at one ply and +0.87 one ply deeper.
- Pricing. An added answer is priced for every listed row of the graded side by one natural draw, and the near rows that can still decide the ranking (at most 2, `ANSWER_DEEP_ROWS`) price it one ply deeper, because the best row's step from one ply to one ply deeper does not carry to the other rows. The side's ranking is then solved over its listed rows against the listed and the added answers. A round that changes a side's best row sends the new row's unverified cells through one more verify round. The score stays the visit mean; the rankings read the answers. A row's floor can then name an added answer, which the matrix has no column for: its tooltip and the matrix dialog say so.

Details: [EVALUATION-DETAILS.md](./EVALUATION-DETAILS.md#the-verify-round-and-the-answer-round).

### Win probability

Choices rank by expected value against the opponent's equilibrium mix. The score lives in win-probability units: one sigmoid fitted to real outcomes applies at the search leaf, so averaging values averages probabilities, which makes variance worth something when you are behind. The mapping is phase-aware, claiming more per point of edge as Pokémon faint: the leaf's K at a fainted share f (fainted over total bodies of both sides) is k0 + k1·f, one pair per family: standard singles 2.28 + 1.49 f, Doubles OU and VGC 2.98 + 0.88 f, Champions doubles 1.86 + 4.17 f, and Champions singles on the standard singles values. Shown percentages pass a second calibration stage fitted on the weight corpus and checked out of sample, because averaging re-inflates the aggregated root score. Its K is one shared constant, 1.85, for the opening of the game, and from a quarter fainted it rises by 5 per unit of fainted share over the next quarter (3.10 at half the bodies fainted): late positions are proven, and the flat constant showed them too timid. A K per mode or per game type did not beat the shared form out of sample. A finished position reads 100/0. The bar of the turn card shows the search's value and the ranked rows show the checked table of choices, so the two can sit a few points apart (the bar's hover text says so; the score stays the visit mean, see the verify round above).

### Matchup, hazard and race terms

- Matchup: a per-pair 1v1 threat estimate (movesets, type chart, stats, speed, big items, immunity abilities, priority, recovery walls) makes early positions readable before anything faints. Active pairs weigh more than bench pairs, and the term reads the doubled speed of a Tailwind side.
- Hazards: priced per living victim, with Boots, Magic Guard, grounding and Toxic Spikes immunity read from the simulator or the type chart. A living hazard remover holds an option on the net board (Defog nets relief minus the hazards it clears off its own side, and a net-negative option is never exercised). A benched Pokémon fights through its entry damage, and one that cannot survive re-entry is priced as finished unless a teammate can clear the hazards.
- Race: with one living Pokémon per side, the leaf gives the race winner 0.6 plus 0.1 per turn of clock margin, capped at 0.9. The clocks come from heal, residual and PP budgets, and mutual walls keep the static. Fixed-damage moves count at their fixed amount.
- Stat drops: a raised stage counts for its holder, and a lowered one counts only while the holder cannot leave the field. A switch clears every stage in the simulator, so a body with a living bench and no trap carries its drop for one turn, not for the game, and reads 0 in the `boosts` term (Doubles OU) and in the boosts-that-bite term (the other tables). A trapped body, or one with no living body off the field, keeps the drop as before. The trap and the bench come from the simulator (`pokemon.trapped`, `battle.canSwitch`), and the drop still weakens the holder's own hits in the damage reads of the turn it stays. The switch itself is priced where the search prices it: the free hit and the hazards on entry.
- Smaller terms: boosts that bite, coverage gaps, Choice items locked into bad moves, status-dampened setup, screens, speed control, and in doubles pair focus and redirection. A tie among the leading rows folds the one-ply horizon trend into their win chance and their order; each row's floor stays its lowest cell.

### The forced-win prover

On a small board (at most three living Pokémon, or the last pair), or when the decided sweep names a side, a prover runs after the root search. It plays the favored side's moves against every reply in the simulator, outcome class by outcome class, until the game ends, and reports the proven mass: the share of classes along which the side wins against everything. A proof at or above one half lifts the score to that mass; the matrix and rankings stay as searched, and the engine's own score stays next to the bar. At 0.9 or above the turn card says "X wins in N against every reply", with a caveat when one roll stays open, a crit was not priced, or the rolls were sampled (doubles). The root tries the ranking's first three moves with the full proof, singles and doubles alike, and the budget is 100 matrix cells per position, never wall clock, so a position always proves the same way. A move that only stalls can spend those cells before the next move is tried; the ranking's first move is the search's best, so the corpus showed no proof lost that way. A class the simulator refuses stays open and lowers the mass. Each of the three root candidates runs a lazy pass first, which leaves the children that a die turned aside and so gives a lower bound of its mass; only a candidate at one half or more gets the full proof. A turn that draws one die more than once (a contact ability, a repeated Protect) or whose draws disagree on the faints is unpriced, and no proof runs through it; where the draws agree, the child carries the chance the simulator passes to its draw, so a repeated Protect splits its cell by the real chance. A proof below mass 1 stays visible from `MIN_FORCED_MASS`, for the side that lost its game too, and the card and the report name its share ("in 98% of the rolls"). A win that a repeated stall only delays says so (round 89): "PsychedelicDawgII wins within 2 turns against every reply on the sampled rolls; a repeated Protect only delays it." The clause ends the sentence in every form (a share, a crit caveat, an open roll), the stall carries the Dex name of the battle's own game (`ForcedWin.delayName`; Detect, Spiky Shield and the rest read as themselves), `turns` counts the delayed turn, and the Game review (switched off in 1.2.0) says the same in the past tense ("only delayed it"). A win through a choice the search's own list never held (a sure strike, doubles only) adds a second sentence that names it: "The winning line is Aqua Jet, which strikes first, with Heat Wave beside it." (`ForcedWin.choiceNames`: the move that moves first by the simulator's queue, then its partner). A result stored without the fields reads as before; one with a `delay` and no name says "a repeated stalling move". A cell the class plan does not cover (every doubles cell, and a singles cell with a stalling move) draws the cell's own seeds and proves only when all draws agree: five in doubles, three in singles (`PLAIN_DRAWS` in [`endgame/children.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/endgame/children.ts)). A doubles proof that rested on three agreeing draws of a roll the simulator holds one time in three is gone with the fifth draw; the probe measured about one proof in five on the doubles holdouts. On boards above three Pokémon the prover waits for the search to read the game at 0.6 or beyond.

A repeated stall that changes nothing is a delay (round 89). A foe that clicks Protect, Detect, Endure or a kin of them again holds with a chance that only falls along a chain (a third, then a ninth; Generations 3 and 4 stop at one eighth), and the chain ends with the move's PP. When the held turn leaves the board as it was, the prover counts the held child as a delay and prices the cell by what the failed repeat gives: the mass of the other children divided by the share they hold. "As it was" is a reading of the simulator's own state ([`endgame/delay-facts.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/endgame/delay-facts.ts)): HP, status, weather and timers, a pending future attack by the turns it has left (Future Sight, Doom Desire), stages, the bodies on the bench and the partner's actions all equal, while the foe's stall counter, the PP the margin covers and the last-move fields that no move, ability or item on the board reads do not count. The rule needs every held child to be a delay and the side's own moves to keep more PP than the foe's stall PP (twice that against Pressure); a child in which the repeat failed and the foe still stands (an attack that missed) is a delay too. A repeat that matters (Leftovers, a poisoned attacker, a contact shield's damage, the foe's partner hitting, a stage raised) keeps the earlier reading: the hold is a position of its own, one level deeper. A cell whose draws split the end (the hold keeps the board, the failure ends the game) stays unpriced on the plain path; in a repeated-stall cell the prover reads it through its recorded dice (`delaySplit` in [`endgame/children.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/endgame/children.ts)), only where the delay rule fires, so no proof runs through a split that no delay explains. A proof that leans on a delay names the stalling move (`ForcedWinProof.delay`, copied to `EvalResult.forcedWin.delay`), and the card and the report print it (`delayClause` in [`prose/phrases.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/prose/phrases.ts)). Two rules read every plain cell: the child of a flipped die is worth the chance of its answer times the chances of the other deciding dice that branch still drew (a failed second Protect that ends the game before a paralysis die is rolled no longer carries the paralysis' chance), and the lazy pass reads a turned child that ended, because it costs no cell and no state.

Why the delay value is sound ([`endgame/delay-rule.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/endgame/delay-rule.ts)). Let W be the least cell mass that the other replies give at the parent, and X the cell's value by the formula above. A delay child has the parent's facts, so the other replies meet the same boards there. It is the parent with another stall counter and one stall PP less; the other children lose the stall volatile, so their positions do not depend on the counter, and their relative shares are the side's own dice. The proof is an induction over the foe's stall PP. With no stall PP left the foe has no stall reply and the cell is worth W. Otherwise the cell is worth at least X(1 - d) + d·min(W, X) >= min(W, X), with d the delays' share, so the parent is worth at least min(W, X), the value its AND node takes. The PP margin keeps the side's own choice legal along the chain, and a held child that is no delay (a heal, a status, the partner's hit, a contact shield's damage, a stage raised) refuses the rule for the whole cell.

A sure strike goes first (round 89). When a move of the side knocks out the foe's last standing body in every roll and moves before every damaging move the foe can click, the game ends before the foe acts, and the only reply that can stop it is a stalling move, which the proof reads as a reply of its own (a repeat that changes nothing is a delay). The root tries such a move before the ranking's three (four candidates), and an inner node tries it before its own two (three); the strike takes no place of a candidate that was there before, because a first form that did cost proofs in the corpus sample. At the root the strike runs on cells of its own (up to one more budget of 100) and gives them back unless it ends the root, so the ranking's candidates meet the 100 shared cells as before the strike (the strike's cells stay cached, and only the extra work shows in the time); `ForcedWinProof.cells` therefore counts the ranking's cells, plus the strike's only when the strike proves the win. `PROVER_BUDGET` keeps its meaning for the ranking's candidates and for every inner node. On the lane's 163 endgame positions the strike raised four proofs (0.667 to 1.0, 0.667 to 0.996, 0.667 to 1.0, 0.667 to 1.0), added one (0.667), removed none and lowered one (1.0 to 0.963); the proofs of the side that lost stayed at 12. It costs prover time: 13 of the 163 roots, those with a strike that does not end the root, draw its refunded cells once more, so the mean prover time per position rose by 3 to 7 % in singles and Doubles OU, 14 to 17 % in VGC, 44 to 46 % in Champions VGC and 17 to 20 % over all 163 positions (read in both run orders of one process; the cold first run biases a single order by about 12 points). The prover reads the three facts from the simulator, never from a list of moves ([`endgame/prover-strike.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/endgame/prover-strike.ts)): the damage calculator's odds say every roll and crit knocks the body out; the simulator's queue on a copy of the battle says the move acts before each damaging foe move (the Dex priority after every modifier, then the action speed, so Trick Room, Tailwind and paralysis count; the foe's chances answer yes, so a Quick Claw that may fire takes the strike away; an equal place is a speed tie the queue shuffles and is no strike); and the strike turn, played on the seeds of a plain cell against each damaging foe move, ends the game in the side's win with no foe move in the turn's log (Psychic Terrain under a grounded foe, Dazzling and its kin, a first-turn move used again and a Focus Sash all stop it there). The candidate comes from the side's legal choices, not only from the engine's list: in doubles it can be a combination the search never ranked (Heat Wave beside Aqua Jet in Champions VGC 2682637901 turn 6). When a proof stands on such a choice it names it (`ForcedWinProof.choice`, copied to `EvalResult.forcedWin.choice`; the card prints it as "The winning line is ..." through `choiceSentence`); a choice the search's list holds leaves the field out. A foe with a second body standing gets no strike (its partner's moves, a redirection and a switch stay in play, and the ranking's order serves those boards), and Generations 1 and 2 get none (the model has no kill odds there). The card can replay a proof as a line: the toggle "How does <player> win from here?" described under [Win paths in a lost endgame](#win-paths-in-a-lost-endgame).

### Weight tables

Each rule set and game type reads its own static weight table, named from the replay's format ([`weights.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/score/weights.ts)). The fits use a pinned corpus of about 5,600 tournament and ladder games built the way the app builds teams, and the doubles and Champions fits are checked on a held-out third of their games.

- Standard singles: the hand-set table (matchup 120, screens 5, tailwind 8, Trick Room 10), with a phase form on two terms ([`score/phase-form.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/score/phase-form.ts)): a weight that runs with the game reads `base + addend x the share of fainted bodies`, and standard singles takes it on bodies (148 + 232 x share) and matchup (137 + 327 x share). `statusFix` weighs what `bodies` weighs, so a body whose ability changes what a status costs is priced at the phase weight of bodies. The other families keep their tables, and `EVAL_STATIC_FORM=0` in Node gives the table's score back exactly.
- Standard doubles (Doubles OU): fitted; screens 88, Tailwind 68, Trick Room 58, matchup 118.
- VGC doubles (Scarlet/Violet VGC): fitted on its own games; Tailwind 0, because the matchup term already reads the doubled speed, Trick Room 54, screens 48, matchup 169.
- Champions singles: a body counts 121 against 200 in standard singles, and a matchup 210 against 120.
- Champions doubles and Champions VGC: fitted on rated Champions games; screens 83, matchup 96, Trick Room 87.

### Other engines

The Evaluation panel can hand positions to another engine instead of the search ([ENGINES.md](./ENGINES.md)). The dashboard sends it the options the search would rank and checks the shape of every answer. An answer with the value of every pair goes through the same equilibrium solve as the search's matrix; an answer with one value per option stands as the engine gave it, so floors and punishers stay empty. Verdicts, the graph and the report read those values with the same bands. The bank, the holdouts and the expert pins measure the dashboard's own search, so another engine's percentages carry that engine's calibration; the panel says "estimate from" and the engine's name, and the Setup menu hides depth and samples while another engine evaluates.

## 3. Game analysis

### The three-pass sweep

`Analyze game` turns the game into an evaluation graph. A background sweep runs three passes: a depth-1 scan shapes the line in seconds, your settings then deepen every report-worthy swing, and the whole line converges to your settings. The header counts each pass in its own words: "turn 3/11" for the scan (from turn 1), "key turns 2/7" for the swing turns and their neighbours (`selectKeyTurns`), "other turns 2/4" for the rest (`SweepProgress.task`, `sweepTurns`). Per-turn d1, d2 and MCTS badges show where each turn stands. Results cache per turn in IndexedDB, merge across partial sweeps and serve single-position evaluations; a stored result never downgrades to a shallower one. The cache key carries an engine version, and any change that moves what a position evaluates to bumps it, so old results never pass as current.

The graph draws the win-probability line with markers on the turns whose play created each blunder-sized swing; clicking a point opens that turn. "On load" starts the sweep when a replay loads and evaluates the newest variation position without a button; after a first Evaluate in a variation, "Follow" evaluates each position you play or step to, and the engine walk and Play line switch it on. Both persist and are off by default. The graph header offers "Re-analyze" only after a sweep of the whole game, and reads "analyzing…" with a turn count only while the sweep reports one. Turn 0 grades the team-preview decision: every lead (singles) or lead pair (doubles) is searched and shown as a diamond before turn 1. A turn the sweep has not reached offers "Analyze this position".

### Turn verdicts

Each turn shows what each player played against the engine's choice, with the regret and, from depth 2, the line that prevents it. The swing splits into a decision part and a chance part (rolls, crits). A plain-language sentence leads, then aligned worst-case rows for played against best.

Verdict bands sit on equilibrium-EV regret in win probability: inaccuracy at 5 points, mistake at 10, blunder at 20. On a card, the size of an inaccuracy, mistake or blunder is the difference of the two win chances printed beside it, so the numbers on the card add up; above a regret of 1 the chip names both chances. Beside a read (risk unpunished or paid off) the chip still reads the grade's own pair, the engine's best line against the played line, and the safe row beside it shows its floor as "at worst". The grade rests on the regret on the score scale. Every place that names a grade's size prints the card's number: the Game report's misplay chips and the "seeds of the loss" in its text read it through `regretText` (a misplay carries the pair as `span`), its T0 lead chips through `leadGradeSize`, while sums over turns, payoffs, swings and luck stay in linear points (one win-probability unit spans 50 points). The lead cards size their grade the same way, and so does the bracket behind a ranked row in the Lines list (its printed chance against the top row's; a row that prints the top row's chance has none). Once a side stands at 85% or better, verdicts soften one tier, so garbage time does not stack blunders. Every flagged mistake or blunder is re-searched one depth deeper; the deeper look can acquit a move, never convict it. The re-search and the probes below run for every engine mode.

- Sacrifice: a faint from 15% HP or less is "a low-cost trade", softened one tier and shown as a gray chip. A healthy Pokémon switched in and fed is a simplification sack only while the engine's scores call the game decisively won on both sides of it. A Pokémon that stayed in and died above 15% is a deliberate feed only when the realized outcome hit the line's priced floor and its payoff inside the payoff window clears the safe guarantee. No label forgives a blunder-sized throw.
- Stay-in: a side KO'd before it acted is priced through a stand-in that chose some priority-0 move, so the turn grades the stay-in against the best escape. The order the log shows bounds that stand-in: a move that certainly stands ahead of an action before the fall would have run first, so it is out, and so is a switch (`ruledOutMoves` in [`branch/action-bound.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/branch/action-bound.ts), read from the simulator's priority and action speed; the bound abstains where the log's own order contradicts the queue). Doubles take the same bound for a slot that fell before it acted: the played combination is the best of the rows that fit the log, and the rows the bound rules out for that slot (a move that would have run first, a switch) are not among them, so Extreme Speed is no longer graded for a Dragonite that Floette's Dazzling Gleam knocked out first (#108). The search reads the bound once per root, with the root battle, and ships it on the result as `playedBounds`, next to the kill odds and the unanswered profile: a tree, its merge, both matrix paths and the HTTP engine path carry it, and the grading, the verify round and the sweep's played-pair match read it from there. A turn cached before the field existed has no bound and grades as it did. Sleep, flinch and full-paralysis turns keep a no-blame verdict. The stand-in is the engine's pricing, never a play (round 89): every place that shows the side says "knocked out before it moved, so the log shows no choice" (the turn card, the game review where it is on, the win-path panel and the Game report's misplay row and loss seed, which take the words from `NEVER_ACTED_WORDS` and `neverActedText`), the card's sentences name that side by the words and never credit it with a played line ("Alpha played the engine's preferred line." where only Alpha moved), and the matrix dialog marks no played row or column for it. Doubles name the slot instead (next bullet).
- No logged action (doubles, round 91, #195): a slot whose body fell before it acted, or that the game's end cut off, has no choice in the log. The parse marks the second case beside the fall mark (`PlayedTurn.ended`: a slot unsettled when `|win|` or `|tie|` came), and the analysis lists each such slot with its reason in `noChoiceSlots` (`fell` or `ended`; [`noChoiceSlots`](./packages/eval-engine/src/turn-analysis/played-match.ts) counts a slot only for a body on the field, so the empty slot of a side with one body is none). The played combination stays the best row that fits the slot that moved, so regret, best, safe and tier do not change, and the unmoved slot is the engine's stand-in (`standsIn`, true for this and for the singles stand-in): no risk label and no sack praise rest on it. The card prints the moves that were played with their win chance, then "Orthworm was knocked out before it moved, so the log shows no choice" (or "Volcarona was cut off by the end of the game, so the log shows no choice"), and never the stand-in's label; the Game review row reads the same, and the matrix dialog marks no played row or column for that side. A side with no logged action on any body prints the reasons alone. The prose note after a clause names the reason where it said "Partner's action hidden". A slot with no reason in the log (Ally Switch, an unlisted cause) still reads "partner unseen". The Explanation sentences, the Lines and the Game report's misplay row still print the stand-in's label.

- Risk: a flagged regret whose punisher the opponent never clicked is "risk (unpunished)", not a misplay. Where the matrix shows that the played line did not beat the safe line by one shown point against the reply that came, the card says "a risk that went unpunished" and names the gap: "Against it the safe <line> was worth +N% more", or "did as well" where the gap rounds to 0%. Such a risk earns no read credit: a mistake or blunder counts as that side's decision, and a gamble or an inaccuracy that kept ground reads the same sentence in the note slot. A side that lost ground keeps the card it had.
- Read: an actual pair that beat the safe line's guarantee by a clear margin is a "read paid off", and its payoff may cash in over the following turns. The credit needs a second test on the same matrix: against the reply that came, the played choice must beat the safe choice by at least one shown point (`CLICK_EDGE_MIN` in [`turn-analysis/clicked-cell.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/turn-analysis/clicked-cell.ts)); the card then ends "…came instead, and against it the play beat the safe line by +N%." A result without a matrix, a hidden slot of the opponent's click in doubles, or a played choice equal to the safe one skips the test and keeps the guarantee test alone. The "safe" line is framed as a guarantee, not as "better".
- Quiet turn: the estimate moved less than a mistake band and no one earned a grade. The card says "A quiet turn." (or "A quiet turn: both sides played the engine's preferred line.") only when nothing else stands on it. Beside another sentence (an equilibrium switch, an unanswered entry, a decided or predictive sentence) or a hindsight hint on a side row, it says "Neither side made a mistake." ("Both sides played the engine's preferred line." where both did, "<name> played the engine's preferred line." where the other side never acted) and the badge reads "no mistake" (a side with a mistake or blunder tier, or a turn without played actions, keeps the badge "quiet turn"). A card that names an unpunished risk ("a risk that went unpunished") or an inaccuracy ("was an inaccuracy") writes neither quiet sentence: it starts with that concrete sentence after the estimate, and the badge reads "risk (unpunished)" or "small inaccuracy" (the risk wins where both appear; `quietVoice` returns `risk` or `inaccuracy`). A side with a mistake or blunder tier makes the quiet sentence false, so the card writes none there. Without played actions the card writes no sentence beside another one. The attribution stays `quiet`: pins, key moments and the census read the field, not the words.

Sensitivity probes check a surviving verdict against the opponent's next plausible items, rule-outs respected. If some item flips it, the verdict softens to the most charitable tier and says so ("hinges on Heatran's item (Choice Scarf: mistake · Leftovers: fine)"), because a grade that depends on hidden information is not a grade.

The matrix behind a turn opens from the turn card (`Open matrix`, the overview keeps its own `Matrix` toggle) as a dialog that reads what the card reads. The played row and column carry the word "played" and an outline, the engine's best row and column the word "engine", every header its equilibrium weight (0 % included), and each player's worst cell the word "worst": the cell [`worstReply`](./src/lib/worst-reply.ts) names for the played choice, lowest in Player 1's row and highest in Player 2's column, the rule "Opponent reads my move" reads. A turn with a matrix but no ranked match for a played action marks that row or column as nothing; a result cached before the choice ids existed matches by label, shows no worst cell and stays read-only. A cell click plays the pair out as a variation, as in the overview. A doubles row whose floor names a reply the table lists no column for (an added answer of the answer round) gets one line under the table, six at most and then a count.

### The Read lens

A boundedly rational opponent model (softmax over the opponent's payoffs, anchored to the equilibrium mix, sharpened by the attack and switch tendencies seen in the replay) surfaces a "Read:" line when a confident prediction makes a non-equilibrium choice the better pick, with the payoff per predicted reply. The model is advice and never part of the grade, except that a flagged risk matching it becomes "a read against the opponent's tendencies".

### The game report

Once a sweep covers enough of the game, the report tells the story:

- who won and where the game tipped for good, naming the mechanism only when the report can vouch for it (a paid-off read, or a roll the protocol shows),
- a chess-style accuracy score per player, volatility-weighted, shown once enough decided turns exist,
- "the seeds of the loss": the loser's costliest choices before the tipping point (unpunished risks never count),
- the lead matchup as turn-0 chips, each side's biggest misplays and paid-off reads as clickable chips, and summed regret per player,
- net luck, with a decided game's resolution booked apart from luck,
- the denied early end when the protocol shows a side one roll from clearing everything left and the roll failing,
- the die behind the game's "biggest roll" (the report's own phrase): when the winner's sure kill under 100 % accuracy missed (singles), or a doubles click with a sure kill that names its move and target missed, one sentence gives both chances: "A hit would have left <winner> X% to win; after the miss <winner> stood at Y%." For X the engine searches a copy of the logged turn with that one die turned over (`rolledCopy` in [`branch/rolled-copy.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/branch/rolled-copy.ts)), at the setting of the next turn's card. Without the copy, singles say the miss and Y, and a doubles click stays silent, because the copy finds the die in the log ([`biggest-roll.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/biggest-roll.ts)),
- clickable key moments chosen by a turn's biggest component, so the die behind the biggest roll surfaces even when decision and chance partly cancel.

### Lines

A line is what the search expects after a choice: both players' choices for at most three turns, every die at its likeliest result, and the win chance for the player at its end. Under a turn's two "engine:" rows the Lines section, closed under its `Lines` toggle until the user opens it (like the Explanation, and closed again on every turn; a closed section builds nothing), shows, per player, the engine line (the engine's best choice, the opponent answering with the best reply of the merged ranking among those a tree holds a line against) and the played line (the choice made); a player who played the engine's choice gets one line. A doubles line shows one row per player and step.

- Where a line comes from. Each tree keeps the lines of every root row against its own top three rows of the other side (at most three lines a row, one per answer, the one against its top row first), as long as a node holds 4 visits (`LINE_MIN_VISITS`; the principal variation, which the summary's ", then ..." prose reads, keeps its 8) and for 3 steps at most (`rowLines` in [`search/mcts-node.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/search/mcts-node.ts)). The first step is the row's own cell. Each later step reads its node by value: each side takes its best mean (the first player's highest, the second's lowest, both in the first player's view) among its options with at least 4 visits and a quarter of the node's most visited option, ties going to the more visited option and then to the hint order, and the step is that pair's cell when the cell holds 4 visits, else the node's most visited cell. The decoupled search spreads a node's visits over near-equal options, so its most visited cell can belong to an option with a worse mean (the Roost of turn 25 in 655336 drew more visits than the Dragon Claw that rated higher). The principal variation and the line pass keep the most visited cell, so the summary's prose and the iterations of the pass do not move; no score, rank or grade reads a line. The "then ..." after a ranked row of the panel and the turn card reads the later steps of that row's line in `EvalResult.lines` (`rowLineTail` in [`src/lib/row-line.ts`](./src/lib/row-line.ts), through the same `linesForMode` as the Lines section, so "Opponent reads my move" swaps both), so a card shows one line per row. Each side's best ranked row carries a tail, the later steps of the line the Lines section shows as the engine's line for that row. The other rows, the played row and a row that waits while the other side sends a body in show no "then ...". A result without lines (a matrix search) keeps each row's own `line` there, with the same rule for the waiting row, and the summary's ", then ..." prose reads `RankedChoice.line` too. The Lines section marks a step "(only choice)" where the simulator's request left a side one legal choice: a last body under Encore, Struggle, a recharge or a charge turn. A living bench keeps a switch legal, so it earns no mark. After the prover, the `lines` job replays the line on a battle ([`search/lines.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/search/lines.ts), the donors chosen in [`search/line-donors.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/search/line-donors.ts): every tree's lines of the row, the one whose first answer ranks highest in the merged ranking first, then the heavier first step) and plays each turn with every die at its likeliest result ([`dice/likeliest.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/dice/likeliest.ts)): a chance from one half up happens, a status check goes by its handler, a damage roll takes the middle step (8 of 16, 19 of 39 in Generations 1 and 2), a hit count the likeliest item of the sample, a pick the likeliest option. The job runs on its own worker message, reads no score and writes only the optional `EvalResult.lines`; a failed job leaves the result as it was. It costs 0.6 to 1.1 % of a tree search's CPU in the singles, Doubles OU and VGC samples (measured with one line a row; the walk now reads up to three cells a row and the replay still plays one line a request).
- What it shows. A tree search keeps a line only where a node holds 4 visits (8 until round 89, when a thinner read of the tree was preferred to no line). With 8, a line that goes past the turn's own pair existed for 17 % of the best rows in singles and 35 % in Doubles OU in the first sample, and 8, 8, 25, 60 and 50 % (singles, Champions OU, Doubles OU, VGC, Champions VGC) in the 54 positions of the round's line probe; 4 visits lift those to 25, 50, 30, 60 and 65 %, and the line pass below to 100, 100, 75, 70 and 75 %; a line that stops at an open replacement says so and names the board its end value reads ("The line stops before Tzon sends in a replacement. Its end is the board before the switch-in."). Only tree searches write lines. An early singles turn runs the depth-1 matrix in Auto, which keeps none, so a graded one gets its lines from a second step (see "The early singles turns" below); a turn that no card grades still shows "No line on this turn", a turn the stage has still to search says "No line yet: the app searches this turn after the game report" and, while the sweep runs in Auto, "No line yet: the game analysis is still running", and a turn that another engine evaluated reads "No line: <engine> sends none."
- The line pass. A line stops where the tree gave its second turn fewer than 4 visits, which the tree's own 600 iterations leave on most singles turns, because they go to the cells of the top rows. A host can ask for extra iterations after the search (`EvalSettings.linePass`, 300 per tree in the app; [`search/line-pass.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/search/line-pass.ts)). Once a tree has its result, its cell statistics and its ranking, each pass iteration starts at one of the cells whose line the card shows (the engine's row and the played row of each player, against the tree's top three answers; only cells the tree already holds) and descends by the same decoupled UCB below it ([`search/descent.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/search/descent.ts), the tree's own loop uses the same code). The pass takes the wanted cells in turn until every wanted line holds two turns or the quota is spent. It writes below the root only: the root's arrays, its visit count and every statistic that the result and the merge read stay as the tree left them (a spec holds every field of the tree stats but the lines equal, singles and doubles), so no score, rank or grade moves. A search that is stopped runs no pass. The pass costs tree time: 300 iterations per tree measured at 1.41 times the tree CPU in singles and 1.29 times in doubles on the 54 positions of probe G14a, and the whole-game analysis time of the stage is measured in the ledger of round 89 (lane G, G14b).
- The early singles turns and the stage after the report. Singles in Auto run the matrix below a quarter of the bodies fainted, and doubles run the tree from turn 1, so only singles have turns without lines. After the game report ([`useEarlyLines`](./src/hooks/useEarlyLines.ts), mounted by the app controller) the app searches each turn the report grades, on a matrix result or a tree result alike (a doubles game, VGC and Champions VGC included: their lines from the sweep stand on thin second turns too), with one tree search on the shared pool that asks for the line pass, and keeps only its `lines`; the score, the grade, the rows and every other field of the turn stay the sweep's (`attachLines` in [`early-lines-io.ts`](./src/hooks/evaluation/early-lines-io.ts) replaces the result with itself plus the lines and the mark `linePass`, in the graph, the memory cache and the stored entry, so a reload reads them back and searches nothing again; a search that keeps no line leaves the lines the sweep made). A tree turn runs its trees a second time for this (the search is deterministic, so the trees equal the sweep's) and adds the pass. The search is told the card's own rows: `lineRows` names each side's engine row as the card prints it, `lineReplies` the answer the card ranks first, and the donors meet that answer before the tree's merged ranking ([`search/line-donors.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/search/line-donors.ts)). The graded rule is the review's (`gradedSides` in [`game-review.ts`](./src/components/eval/game-review.ts)); the stage runs in Auto only (an explicit mode keeps the user's choice), one turn at a time, and the Game review shows "Finding lines for graded turns: 2 of 6" with a Stop. A new analysis, a new replay or a Stop ends it, and the next analysis finishes the turns that are left. The feedback harness takes its dump after the stage (`window.__psEarlyLines.active` is false), so two runs of one build dump the same lines. The added time is measured per game on devbox in the ledger of round 89 (lane G, G13).
- A row and a line are two numbers. A row's percentage averages many outcomes of its dice, a line follows one of them, and the lines' ends order the options the other way round from the rows' values on 9 of 19 sides in the singles sample. Neither is wrong; the line is one story the dice can tell, the row is the average over all of them. The Lines section prints both on each line ("row 23%, this line ends at 19%", the row being the engine's or the played one as the card prints it), and when any line ends at another number than its row, one muted sentence under the section says why: "A row's value averages every answer and die the search tried; a line follows one answer with each die at its likeliest result." A row at 100% or 0% is one where every answer and die ends the game, and its line stops before the game ends, so a second sentence follows where such a row stands beside a line that ends elsewhere: "A row at 100% (or 0%) is one where every answer and die ends the game; its line stops before the game ends, so its end is the estimate of the position it stops at." The Game review's lines (where it is on) end with "ends at 19%" beside the row's value in the head, and the same sentences stand once under the review. A win-path line has no row, so it shows its end alone.
- The answer a line meets. The head of a line names the opponent's choice in the line's first step and its place in the merged ranking of the search that holds the lines: "vs Draining Kiss (Tzon's top choice)", "(Tzon's second choice in this search)", "(Tzon's fourth choice in this search)", or "(not among Tzon's ranked choices)" for a doubles pair that the ranking pruned. An answer below the top carries a title that says each tree follows the answer it explored most. The best answer means the best one overall by the merged ranking, never the one a single tree liked most. A line that opens with a replacement meets no answer and names none. The Game review's line text (where it is on) carries the same bracket after its first answer. A doubles replacement reads without the "pass" that the engine's label gives a slot that sends nobody in ("replaces: → Basculegion", not "→ Basculegion + pass").
- The click. `▶ Play` turns the line into a variation: every step's slot choices carry their forced dice (the tooltip lists every one, a chip shows only a die that could have gone the other way; a damage roll that decides a knock-out reads "KO (10 of 16 rolls)" when the forced roll knocks out and "no KO (6 of 16 rolls)" when it does not, with the rolls that give that result), and Let it play out continues from the line's end with the simulator's own seed. A die the click cannot force (a sleep move's duration, a hit count with no range such as Population Bomb's) is named under the line. A random target is named only where its sample can change the turn: more than one foe stands and the move, after its own handlers changed its target, aims at one body (`sampleDecides` in [`dice/aim.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/dice/aim.ts), fed the target of the simulator's last `getRandomTarget` call); the simulator samples a foe for every doubles click without a target, but a spread, field or self-aimed move ignores it, and `dice-random-target-note.spec.ts` plays every handler that rewrites a move's target against the note. A stalling move's chip shows whether the move fails or works again. A variation that is open asks before the click replaces it.
- Opponent reads my move (switched off in 1.2.0: the code stands behind `VITE_FEATURE_OPPONENT_READS`, `opponentReadsEnabled` in [`src/lib/feature-flags.ts`](./src/lib/feature-flags.ts), off in every build; the checkbox, the dock note and the title sentences are not drawn, the evaluation hook hands every reader the preference as unset and keeps a `hardMode` stored by an earlier nightly as it was; the rule below returns in 1.2.1). A checkbox in the Setup menu under "Animate branch turns" (`EvalPreferences.hardMode`, a stored key and an identifier that kept their names) sets who answers: the opponent plays the reply the search rates lowest for the move in hand, not its top choice; a note under the box (`OPPONENT_READS_NOTE` in `SetupMenu.tsx`, shown with the box on and off) says so. One rule, `worstReply` in [`src/lib/worst-reply.ts`](./src/lib/worst-reply.ts) on the engine's `worstAnswer` ([`turn-analysis/worst-answer.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/turn-analysis/worst-answer.ts)): the lowest cell of the move's row among the cells the result trusts (`cellSolid`, the bar of the hindsight read: verified, or 8 visits, or a visited plain cell; a result with no cell statistics, a matrix pass, trusts every cell), else the row's own floor (`punishedBy`, marked "from a thinly searched cell"), else the opponent's best row. In a doubles game the answer is a joint choice; the click walk and the dock fill map it onto the pickers slot by slot through the same `slotChoicesOf` and `pickFillParts` as any engine choice, so a reply that does not map or clashes with a touched slot gives way to the next ranked one. Three places read it. (1) A click on an engine move in the turn card or the evaluation panel hands the result along and the walk plays the reply the search rates lowest for that move; a click on a matrix cell names its pair and is played as clicked. The title of an engine-move button (the turn card's rows, the panel's ranked rows) names that reply with its label (`exploreTitle` and `readsReply` in `turn-copy.ts`: "... the opponent reads your move and answers with Defog.", a joint label in doubles; without a result the title says the opponent plays the reply the search rates lowest for it). The "worst vs" beside the better or safe choice names that reply; where it differs from the row's floor (`punishedBy`) the "worst vs" carries a title (`readsWorstLabel`): the floor rests on a thinly searched cell (`basis` `cell`), or the row's lowest cell matches no ranked reply and the opponent's best row answers (`basis` `best`). The played row's "worst vs" and the "risked ... vs" cell keep `punishedBy`, because the grade's risk reads that floor, and so does the ranked row's "(worst reply: X)" with the box on and off: the "guaranteed at least" number belongs to the reply it names, so the tooltip never swaps in the reply the click plays. (2) The turn dock: the side whose slots the user set is the move in hand when the other side holds no pick of the user's; its joint choice (the user's slots with the partner slot the fill wrote, read as one ranked row by `rowOfChoices`) decides the opponent's reply. The fill runs once per position as before and, with the setting on, fills the side in hand first; a later pick of the user rewrites the opponent's engine slots from the pick's own handler, never from an effect, and a pick on both sides leaves both alone. A change of the setting applies from the next pick; while it is on, the dock's status row starts with "Opponent reads your move" and a "Turn off" button (`DockStatus`) and, in a variation, ends with one of four tails, `DockFillView.reads` (at a replay turn the note stays without a tail, since the replay's own choices stand for both sides): `waiting` (no pick of the user's yet: "its reply follows your pick"), `answered` (a ranked row stands for the pick and the reply is written: "its engine slots answer your pick"), `unranked` (no ranked row stands for the pick, or the reply fits no slot: "no ranked reply to this pick, so its slots keep the engine's choice"; the opponent's engine slots go back to the engine's own fill for the position, the user's own picks on that side stay) and `both` (both sides hold picks of the user's: "both sides hold your picks"); the fill writes the read and a pick commits it from its own handler, and a pick made with the setting off, or "Back to the engine's pick" with it off, leaves it at `waiting`. "Back to the engine's pick" in the tray of a slot that holds a user pick differing from the fill's (`DockFillView.picks`, frozen at the fill) restores that slot's engine choice through `DockFillView.restore`, marks the slot as the engine's again and answers for the opponent: on, its engine slots take the reply to the restored pick (the rule of any pick), in doubles the joint reply recomputed; off, the engine slots the fill wrote return to their fill as at the start; slots the user set are left alone, and a replacement's tray has no button. (3) The lines: beside a row's usual line a tree search holds a line against the row's worst answer where a tree gave that cell 4 visits (`EvalLine.versus: 'worst'`; `rowLines` keeps a floor line apart from the lists the usual donors read, `lineRequests` asks for it only when the usual line meets another answer, and the line pass spends what the plain cells leave of its quota on the floor cells). With the setting on the section opens with one line, "Opponent reads your move: each line meets the reply that hurts its choice most." (`READS_LINES_NOTE` in `line-view.ts`, drawn when the turn has lines; off, none), shows that line in place of the usual one and its head reads "vs Moonblast (the reply the search rates lowest for this choice, 49% in its cell)" (the cell's win percent from the card's matrix for the row's side, printed with the turn's fainted share as the card prints its rows; a floor reply reads "from a thinly searched cell" instead), whatever the choice (a move, a switch, a joint choice in doubles: the line printed it a few words before); a row without one shows today's line and the sentence "No line against Moonblast, the reply the search rates lowest for this choice. This line meets Draining Kiss."; with it off the worst lines are not shown. A pivot's rows in the card are pairs ("U-turn → Keldeo") while a line names the move alone ("U-turn": the follow-up is a step of its own), so the lookup of a bare move reads the best ranked pair row and a reply that is a pair stands for its move (`pivotMove`, `sameAnswer` in `src/lib/worst-reply.ts`; the rank of an answer and the "No line against" sentence read the same way, with the setting on or off). Row tails ("then ...") and the Game review read the usual line whatever the setting, so a row's tail continues the line its percentage belongs to and a pair of the review never mixes two lines. Let it play out is unchanged (both sides are the engine). Grades, scores, the report and the win paths do not read the setting, and the stored lines of a result hold both kinds whatever the setting says.

### The Game review

Release 1.2.0 ships the Game review switched off: the code stands behind `VITE_FEATURE_GAME_REVIEW` (`gameReviewEnabled` in [`src/lib/feature-flags.ts`](./src/lib/feature-flags.ts), off in every build), and with the flag off the section, the graph marks and the legend are not drawn and no sentence names the section. The stage after the report still runs, because the Lines section reads its lines. The section returns in 1.2.1.

Below the game report and below each turn's card, a fold that starts closed and says how many graded turns it holds, the Game review lists, per player, the turns that the cards grade as inaccuracy, mistake or blunder: the played value, the engine's value (or the safe line's floor, worded "at worst", where the card shows a read), the loss (the difference of the played chance and the engine's best line's chance), and the engine line and the played line with a `▶ Play` each. A mistake that the card turns into a sacrifice or a read that paid off is not listed, and neither is an inaccuracy whose read paid off. The graph marks the same turns, a circle for player 1 and a square for player 2. A click on a row's turn shows the turn's card above the review, which stays open and marks the row of the shown turn; the fold keeps its state while the game stays loaded, so a board step, a trip into a variation and a new report leave it as it was. With a variation on screen, "Back to the main line" leads out of it.

### Win paths in a lost endgame

A game that reached an endgame (three Pokémon or fewer standing in total, `ENDGAME_MAX_BODIES`; the count takes the bodies each side brought, so VGC counts four) adds an Endgame row to the Game review for the player who lost (the row exists while the Game review is switched off, but it is not drawn), and the card of every endgame turn carries a closed toggle "How could <player> have won from here?" for that player. The card of any other turn where the loser stands 0.3 or more behind carries it too (`WIN_PATHS_BEHIND` in [`src/lib/endgame-scope.ts`](./src/lib/endgame-scope.ts); the score runs from -1 to +1 from player 1's side, so a player 2 loser reads it with the sign turned). The endgame turns and those turns are the turns offered, and a game with no loser offers none. A click on the row starts the search from the endgame's first turn and opens the panel under the row; the panel's picker moves it to a later endgame turn, and the row and the picker keep the endgame turns only. Opening the card's toggle starts the search for the card's own turn, and a closed toggle starts none. One search runs at a time, and each place (the review, the card) and each question (this one, the proof's line below) shows only the one it opened. The search is an on-request job, never part of a sweep, and it answers one step at a time ([`endgame/win-paths/`](./packages/eval-engine/src/endgame/win-paths/)). It reads a position the app rebuilds, so it needs the same sets as the card, and it writes into no result. The app keeps a finished answer in the browser's store under the turn, its sets and the request, so the same question after a reload answers at once; a failed or stopped search keeps nothing.

- Step 1, "A stronger play". The forced-win prover tries each of the loser's choices at the turn in the card's ranking, with three turns as its depth, and reports for each the share of the dice classes that win whatever the opponent answers. The panel shows the best choice's share beside the played choice's ("wins within 3 turns in 52 % of the dice whatever <opponent> plays. What <loser> played, <choice>, wins in 0 % of the dice."; the choice is written in the game's words: "a switch to Corviknight", "Heat Wave on Ursaluna", "Terastallize and Hydro Pump"). A loser that was knocked out before it moved shows no choice in the log; the panel says so ("Rotom-Wash was knocked out before it moved, so the log shows no choice; staying in wins in 4 % of the dice.", the number of the card's stand-in for that stay). A choice counts as stronger when its share beats the played one's and is above zero. The prover's caveats stand: a crit stays outside the share, and doubles prove over sampled rolls ("over sampled rolls" in the panel). The line is one witness: the likeliest dice under which the proof's choices win.
- Step 2, "Luck". Both sides play the engine's first choice, and a best-first search over the dice finds the likeliest sequence of dice under which the loser wins within three turns. The engine's first choice is the turn's ranking at the first turn and, at every later position, the best row of the one-turn matrix against the other side's equilibrium mix ([`endgame/win-paths/first-choice.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/endgame/win-paths/first-choice.ts)). The panel shows the dice as chips and the chance of that sequence ("This sequence: 10.8 %"). A sequence below 1 in 1,000 is not searched: "None within 3 turns at 1 in 1,000 or better." `▶ Play` forces those dice, so the variation ends in the win under any seed.
- Step 3, "A mistake by <opponent>". Both sides play the engine's first choice with every die at its likeliest result. At each turn of that line the opponent's lower-ranked choices are the candidates, tried mildest first (smallest loss against its first choice, then the earlier turn, then the better rank); the first one after which the loser wins within three turns is the answer ("If <opponent> had played <choice> (its second choice) on turn N, <loser> wins: this sequence has 84 %", one sentence; later ranks read "third choice", "fourth choice", up to "tenth choice", then "11th choice"), with the chance of the dice the line needs. A line allows one slip, and a replacement after a faint is never one.
- What the numbers mean. Each number is a lower bound. The stronger play's share is the prover's mass under its class model; the luck and mistake chances belong to the one sequence shown, so other sequences may add to the chance of winning. A sequence's chance multiplies the dice whose other result would change who faints (`chanceOfDie` in [`endgame/win-paths/luck-path.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/endgame/win-paths/luck-path.ts)); a die that changes nothing counts as certain, although its chip may still show a chance, and a luck chance can read below the stronger play's share because the share adds every winning sequence of a choice and the luck path prices one sequence of the engine's first choice. One muted line under the steps says so. When a path ends with both last bodies fainting in one turn, the simulator names the winner (a tie before Generation 5, the side whose body fell last from Generation 5; `win-paths-double-faint.spec.ts` plays it by generation and cause), and `wonBy` reads that and nothing else. All three steps of the loser's question hold within three turns. A step stops at its search limit, counted and never timed so that it answers the same on every machine: 350 matrix cells or 175 simulator turns (`WIN_BUDGET` in [`endgame/win-paths/budget.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/endgame/win-paths/budget.ts)), about 10 to 20 s on the devbox where a step runs into it. A step that stopped says "Stopped at the search limit; nothing found so far." and one that covered every path says "None within 3 turns." A loser's question spends up to three of those caps. In the probe over 54 lost endgames of the corpus, step 1 finished inside the limit on every singles, Champions OU, Doubles OU and VGC position and on 91 % of Champions VGC, and step 2 on 92 % of singles and 90 % of Champions OU and Doubles OU positions; step 3 was not probed on that corpus.
- The proof's line. A turn whose result carries a forced win (the prover's proof, a mass of one half or more) adds a second closed toggle, "How does <player> win from here?", for the side the proof names, whoever won the game and whether or not the card speaks of the forced win. Opening it runs the job with one step: the stronger play, at the proof's own number of turns, which can run past three, in the prover's root order, with the proof's choice first and, where the proof wins through a sure strike that the search's list lacks, that strike as the choice (`proof` on `WinPathsRequest`, [`endgame/win-paths/stronger.ts`](./packages/eval-engine/src/endgame/win-paths/stronger.ts)). The step shows its line even when the player played that choice ("<player> played this choice."), where the loser's step answers none when the played choice is as strong as the best. When the played choice wins in as large a share of the dice as the proof's choice, the line opens with the one that wins within fewer turns, and at equal turns with the played choice; the sentence counts the turns of the choice it names. Its chance is at least the card's mass, and one open spends one cap. The panel is titled "How does <player> win?" with the step "The proof's line", has no picker and no sequence note, and a proof that the replay does not reach within its turns says "The proof did not replay within n turns."; doubles read "over sampled rolls". The job keeps no field of the result: it finds the proof again with the same prover, and its finished answer is stored under a key of its own, because the request differs.

Details: [EVALUATION-DETAILS.md](./EVALUATION-DETAILS.md#win-paths-in-a-lost-endgame).

### How far one grade can be trusted

Two analyses of one turn with different search dice can give a side different tiers. Measured in round 89 on 63 sampled turns (122 sides, five dice salts per side, weighted to the population): the chance of another tier between two salts is 9.2 % on the round's base and 10.2 % at its head (pooled; singles 7.0 % to 9.3 %, doubles 19.6 % to 14.3 %). Among the sides that show a tier at the dice the app uses, the chance of another tier under another salt is 47 % (44 % on the base). The sample ran on 11.11.2026, before the round's later changes to the search and the grading, so the rates describe that head and are not a measurement of the shipped one. One analysis is one draw of the dice. More draws for the graded side would lower the rate; that is a proposal with its cost in search time still to measure.

## 4. Timeline and playing lines

The replay is the main line. Executing a different move at any turn opens the single variation there, and the T0 view lets you pick leads (one per side in singles, both slots in doubles) and "Play from turn 0". Navigation is view-only and lossless. Playing a different move inside the variation cuts its tail; replacing the variation from the main line asks first, and `Discard variation` asks once the variation holds a played turn ("The variation has N played turns. Discarding drops them.", with the confirm banner under the timeline bar; singles and doubles count played turns alike). The graph overlays the variation as a gold curve. Branches save locally and share by link: a version 2 link carries a game's teams, seed and played choices and opens the game with its open variation at the sharer's turn (ADR 0014).

A new game is a main line of its own: `Game`, then `New game`, takes two teams and a format, opens at Team Preview (or at turn 0 where the format has none), and grows turn by turn as the user or the engine plays. Both sides know both teams in full, as with open team sheets, and the user plays both sides. The game is a record in the browser's storage (the format, both teams, the seed and every turn played), saved at Start and after every finished turn; the address names it (`?game=<id>`), so a reload opens it again. Its dice come from the game's seed, which the dialog shows and can set; a variation plays the game's own dice up to the change and what the seed gives after it. The app rebuilds the game's log from the record, so grading, the report, the lines and variations work as on a replay.

"Let it play out" has the engine play both sides' top choice to the end; Stop works anytime and every turn stays navigable. Clicking one engine line plays that turn out chess-style, the other side answers with its top reply (with "Opponent reads my move" on, a setting that 1.2.0 ships switched off, with the reply the search rates lowest for the clicked move, see that entry under Lines), and the result re-evaluates. Mid-turn KOs resolve with the engine's replacement.

Bring-limited formats like VGC pick the bring-four at T0, and every reconstruction fields only what was brought. The brought bodies come from the log's `switch`, `drag` and `replace` lines, which also finds a body that entered and fainted within one turn. The trim is both sides or neither: if a side's list does not resolve, both teams stay whole. A side whose log shows fewer bodies than the bring count is a short side (a game that ended before its last brought body entered: 12 % of the corpus games in Scarlet/Violet VGC, 13 % in Champions VGC best of one, 18 % in best of three). The engine plays its fielded bodies plus one guess per missing body from the team preview, takes each set of guesses as a world, searches every world in full and blends the turn's score and each choice's value by the guess's usage share. It keeps the three likeliest worlds, which costs about 35 to 42 % more search time in the families with short sides (ADR 0024). A random format fills the missing bodies from its random-set data (`data.pkmn.cc/random`), six against six.

A deviation rebuilds the simulator at that point from the protocol, the team model, optional usage fallbacks, an HP and status correction at each turn boundary, and a forced turn. The dice the log names (misses, crits, hit counts, secondary and self effects, status checks, protect chains, speed ties) play as logged, and the dice it hides (damage rolls, sleep turns) come from a frozen seed (index 0 of the list, another seed of the list on a seeded turn). A turn whose faints still differ from the log's tries the damage-roll steps of the hits on those bodies, then seeds 1 to 15 for the dice no name covers. A turn that the simulator cannot play forced, or that would end a game the log goes on past, falls back to the search over sixteen seeds. When the guessed sets cannot replay a line (the simulated game wedges or ends early), the branch shows a divergence notice with a pointer to Edit Player/Opp. A variation that ends the game itself raises no notice (round 89): the notice reads the rebuild, and an ended battle with the variation's turns replayed on top is the variation's own end.

The choices the rebuild replays are read from the protocol with these rules (round 89):

- A `|move|` line that another effect wrote (`[from]` Magic Bounce, Dancer, Magic Coat, Snatch) is not the body's own click, and a failed move whose target the log hides gets the aim that makes the simulator fail it as the log shows.
- The stand-in move of a body that never acts, or whose `|cant|` line stands before a logged action, follows the simulator's priority and action speed, so the turn keeps the log's order.
- A revive line answers with the body the log revives, and forced replacements fill the slots in the order the log shows, so a Ditto copies the foe it copied. A body that leaves the field by a correction keeps none of its volatiles.
- A choice the simulator would refuse (a move the rebuilt body lacks, a Disable or an Encore the request refuses) gets a stand-in from the request, a move of the same kind, so the turn still plays.
- Each move slot carries the PP the log shows spent (`PokemonSnapshot.ppUsed`, read from the client's counts and set at every boundary), so a move at 0 PP never ranks first.
- A forme finds its slot by the name the simulator shows the log (Greninja-Bond, Rockruff-Dusk), no longer by a shared name prefix.

## 5. Measuring the engine

Adopting a weight, a search rule or a team-building change goes through a paired gate: the old and new engine run the same bank positions, and the verdict reads paired Brier and log-loss deltas with a 90% band from a bootstrap over replays, where pooled rows decide and single phase cells only warn. Weights also face the holdout. The commands below write only to gitignored folders.

- Bench: `npm run bench:eval` (`scripts/eval-bench.vt.ts`, run by `vitest.bench.config.ts`, outside the suite) prints forward-model and search throughput to the console.
- Calibration bank: `node scripts/run-calibration.mjs [--slices 6] [--out .calibration/<name>]` runs [`test/regression/eval-calibration.spec.ts`](./test/regression/eval-calibration.spec.ts) (`EVAL_CALIBRATION=1`) in parallel slices over 130 pinned replays (Gen 9 OU, Doubles OU, VGC and a few drafts, tournament and ladder) and writes per-slice dumps, `merged.jsonl` and `summary.txt` there. It scores sign accuracy, confidence calibration, per-phase Brier and the win-probability fit, plus an `hq:` line (tournament and 1700+ ladder games) and a `luck-adjusted:` line. Levers include `EVAL_CALIBRATION_DEPTH`, `_MODE=mcts`, `_SAMPLES`, `_TRANCHE`, `_SMOGON=1` (usage fills, disk-pinned in `.smogon-cache/`) and `_SOURCE=fit|holdout` (the fit corpus or the holdout instead of the bank, which brings in Champions). The bank builds teams the way the app does. `node scripts/paired-calibration.mjs a.jsonl b.jsonl [--quality hq|std]` compares two dumps.
- Fit corpus and holdout: `node scripts/build-fit-corpus.mjs` pins about 5,600 replays in `test/regression/fixtures/fit-corpus-manifest.json` (logs cache in `.fit-corpus/`). `EVAL_FIT=1 npx vitest run test/regression/eval-fit.spec.ts` regresses feature weights per rule set and game type with cluster-bootstrap errors, folds clustered by player set, and a phase-conditioned win-probability fit; reports land in `.fit-corpus/`. A third of the doubles and Champions sets (`test/regression/fixtures/fit-holdout-manifest.json`) never enters a fit and serves as a test bed. The harness only reports; weights are adopted by hand after the paired gate.
- Endgame truth: a solver under `packages/eval-engine/src/endgame/` computes the exact value of endgames with at most three living Pokémon, singles and doubles, under best play, by recursing over turn boundaries with chance priced as outcome classes. A result is `exact` only when no cap (30 turns, 20,000 states, 120 s), unpriced chance or loop was touched. `EVAL_ENDGAME_TRUTH=1` grades the static, matrix and MCTS estimators against it on positions the bank exports (`EVAL_CALIBRATION_POSITIONS=<dir>`) and on synthetic fixtures; `node scripts/endgame-truth-table.mjs` renders the dump. The solver is an instrument, not a production path.
- Feedback drift suite: `npm run test:feedback` analyzes seven pinned replays (plus five census replays that feed `scripts/tier-census.mjs`) through the real app and grades them against a corpus distilled from an experienced player's review. It is on demand, hermetic (recorded replays and Smogon data), deterministic on one browser build, and warn-only: drift is an analysis point, never a failing test, and only harness breakage fails. It writes `docs/reports/feedback-drift.md` and `.json` locally (`docs/` is not in the repository); `FEEDBACK_DUMP=1` also writes the full dumps for a byte-level comparison. `node scripts/feedback-ci.mjs --name <name> --runs 3` runs the suite three times in GitHub Actions, one runner per run, and compares the twelve dumps byte for byte (the drift reports compare without their date and wall times); `.calibration/feedback-<name>/run<i>/` holds the downloaded files. The report's alignment line says how many turns of each rebuild match the log (`perfect 37/38` for one replay) with the counts that miss it: `soft`, `faint-residual N`, `seeded N` (a turn that plays under a seed other than the first) and `fallback N` (a turn that took the sixteen-seed search); every count except `soft` shows only when above 0.
- Scene pins: `packages/eval-engine/test/tree-orchestrator.verify-scenes.spec.ts` grades three corpus turns (Gen 6 OU 648453 turn 13, Gen 8 OU 573756 turn 73, Doubles OU 912045 turn 8) and pins each side's tier and regret to a band around a Monte-Carlo reference: every verified tree cell re-priced with 24 draws (40 for 648453) one ply deeper on two seed blocks, which prices its leaves with the engine's static evaluation, so a weight change recomputes the references before a pin is called wrong. The boards are the ones the app builds: `test/regression/verify-scenes-boards.spec.ts` rebuilds each through `bankTeamsFor` (the bank's door to `buildAppTeams`) over the saved replay and Smogon files of the feedback harness and fails when it differs from the fixture under `packages/eval-engine/test/fixtures/verify-scenes/`, naming the first field that moved (`UPDATE_VERIFY_SCENES=1` writes the fixtures again; a moved board moves the pins at the gate). The rows search as the app's Auto mode does (`autoTurnSettings` of the board's fainted share): the tree on 573756 and 912045, the depth-1 matrix on 648453. The scene rows skip the app's deeper re-search of a flagged side (`verifyFlagged`), which only acquits, so a tier a row shows can be one the card later clears.

The always-on suites (`npm run test:regression` runs four Vitest projects: app, replay-core, eval-engine and ui) pin behavior and list themselves; README names the commands.

## 6. Known limits

The app does not recreate the original battle frame by frame, and branches can diverge from the replay when hidden information matters.

- Hidden moves, EVs, IVs, natures and some items and abilities are guesses when the replay did not reveal them. Some Tera details stay unknown before the reveal. Fitted spreads prove consistency with the replay, not the true spread (section 1).
- Usage guesses rest on `data.pkmn.cc` and its mirror. When neither has data for a format, set assumptions from `@pkmn/smogon` apply, then default simulator values. A Random Battle fills the bodies the log never showed from its random-set data (`data.pkmn.cc/random`); the unrevealed fields of the bodies it did show stay `unknown`.
- Choice reconstruction reads `|move|` and `|switch|` lines and translates doubles targets to simulator targets. Redirection, retargeting after a faint and phazing are covered, but unusual targeting effects and volatile state the snapshot never shows can still break or skew a reconstruction. HP and status are corrected at each turn boundary, yet other hidden or transient state may differ. An unplayable reconstruction is detected and reported.
- The rebuild forces the dice the log shows, among them every hit of a multi-accuracy chain such as Triple Axel's, the random target and the drag. Three kinds of result stay with the hidden dice: a proc the log shows (a burn from Flame Body, a Focus Band) can fail under the frozen seed, Present's power is a pick no die kind names, and Diamond Storm's self effect against two foes is not read from the log yet. A body the log knocked out where the simulator's damage roll leaves it standing gets a search from the faint step; a turn that no step set and no seed repairs stays a named residual (the report's `faint-residual`). A knockout that only the simulator plays can leave the rebuilt side with more living bodies than the log shows until a snapshot corrects it.
- Chance in the search: Generations 1 and 2 have no class plan. A multi-hit move whose entry draws at every hit (Twineedle, Double Iron Bash), a random-target move in doubles, a self effect on a kill and a flinch pre-empted by another status in the same hit stay with the seeds. The merge trusts no class pool of a single tree, so a rare class that one tree played deep keeps the one-ply value of the verify round, even where that tree reads far from it. A rule that let a thin pool stand on its standard error was built in round 89 and reverted; the rule that decides when a single tree may stand is open work. A die of a quarter or more reaches at least one tree through the root quantiles; the pool of a class that a single tree played stays thin.
- Damage previews match the simulator's generation, sets and field, and apply an armed Tera toggle. Mega Evolution, Ultra Burst and Z-Moves are left out of previews until the turn executes.
- A share link of version 1 is a compact branch report (identity, format, players, branch turn, executed choices, final log) and does not replay an alternate line. A link of a game (version 2) does: the reader rebuilds the game on its own simulator from the teams and the seed and plays the entries, the open variation included. A link made on another simulator version can fail on a late entry, and the page names the turn.
- A variation lives in the page. A reload and the browser's back button drop an unsaved variation without a question; only `Save Branch` or a share link keeps it, and `Start`, loading a replay or opening a saved game ask before they close one. A set edited in the editor of a replay is kept in the browser per replay like an import and comes back whole after a reload, with the guesses as they stood at the save, even when the usage data has moved since; the app has no button yet that drops a replay's saved edits and imports.
- A drop reads the trap of the last turn end. On a position that stands at an open mid-turn request (the root of a forced replacement), a trap set in that turn is not read yet, and a trapper that left in it still counts. The cells the search prices end at a turn boundary, where the reading is current.
- A line is one outcome of the dice. Its end value and a row's value can order the options differently ("Lines" in section 3), and a tree keeps a line only as deep as its nodes hold 4 visits.
- The engine is a heuristic: a score is not a proof outside the prover's budget, and a search with a fixed budget can miss a line a deeper one finds.
- The replay viewer, sprites and the viewer script load from Pokemon Showdown-hosted assets, and usage stats and set files need the network, so the app is not self-contained; a file-based replay still loads offline except for those assets. On phones the viewer scales its scene down to the frame's width and stands the controls and the log under it.
