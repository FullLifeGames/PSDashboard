# A forced-win prover plays the position out after the root search and lifts the score by the proven mass

Where the board is small (up to three living bodies, or the last pair) or the decided sweep or the near stage names a side and the search already reads it at `PROVER_SCORE_FLOOR` or beyond, the app plays each answer through the simulator to the end of the game after the root search and says "X wins in N against every reply", with the dice share in the sentence ("if the 95 % Fire Fang lands", "barring a crit"). The proof is an AND/OR search over the children that the endgame solver builds (`endgame/`), with a memo on the endgame key and a budget counted in matrix cells (`PROVER_BUDGET`: 100 cells per position, 200 states, depth 12, no wall clock, so results stay deterministic; since round 89 the root tries the ranking's first three moves with the full proof, singles and doubles alike, and no greedy probe gates them). The proven mass (the share of dice outcomes along which the side wins whatever the opponent answers) lifts the score: `score = mass * sign + (1 - mass) * openValue`, where the open branches keep the static value. Matrix, ranking and interval stay as the search left them. A mass of exactly 0.5 (a coin flip: both sides prove one half) stays on the result but moves no score (gate 05.09.2026). The solver that supplies the children also serves as a ground truth for small endgames in the bank tooling (the synthetic endgames of `ENDGAME_FIXTURES`, `endgame-truth.spec.ts`); it does not run in the app. In round 35 the prover cut the Brier of the search against the 14 exactly solved positions from 0.0632 to 0.0243, and proved 33 of 816 bank positions, 30 of them right; the three proofs that lost (2663114316 turns 18 and 22, 751443 turn 23) were wrong in the search before. Chance is priced as outcome classes with one representative child per class, as in the matrix cells.

## Considered options

- A clamp that pins a decided score to plus or minus 0.85 (the "Decided-Klammer"): rejected, because the decided side wins only 80 %. The same clamp (floor 0.85) was built and reverted in round 15, re-measured in round 50 under a hold rule (about 2 bp against a bank resolution of 23 bp) and in T55 (at most 3 bp), and the triage discarded it.
- A state budget instead of a cell budget: the first budget cost 2 s per failed position on the bank, up to 20 s with nine answers. The round's lesson: a state budget does not measure cost, cells do.
- A one-seed probe, an early exit while the static stands, a cost-estimate gate and 150 cells: all measured in round 35 and dropped (too optimistic, or they cost proofs, or no change).
- A greedy five-turn probe (16 cells for the three root candidates) that decided whether the full proof starts: it was part of round 35's design and dropped in round 89 (A5f-6, A5f-7). It missed 8 to 20 % of the doubles proofs the full proof finds (one to three turns long, all for the side that won), because a doubles root has 6 to 16 replies and the shared 16 cells paid for about one turn of one candidate; singles missed none, and the full proof for the three candidates costs 0.3 to 1 % more search forks.
- The static estimate alone in the endgame: it read the wrong side on 749828 turn 23, which the prover corrected.
- Hand lists for the defending side: excluded; the defender gets all legal answers.

## Consequences

The prover fits only the class model (hit, miss, kill roll, tie order): crits are left out of the model and some defensive lines stay open, so 573756 turn 138 proved at 0.88 in round 35 and stayed under the speaking threshold (`SPOKEN_MASS`). Doubles fall back to sampled rolls: the plain path draws the cell's own seeds and books a proof only when every draw agrees. Three agreeing draws booked a Protect that holds one time in three as a sure failure; since round 89 doubles draw five and singles keep three, one table in `endgame/children.ts`. The disclosed limit: five draws that all miss a roll the simulator holds one time in three still happen in 32 of 243 cells (three draws: 8 of 27), and a singles cell with a stalling move keeps the three-draw limit until a measurement names another count. The score changes in endgames, so the cache version bumped in round 35 (v41).


## Amended (round 89)

Round 89 moved four rules of the prover (#59, #145; the lane's step names are A5f and A6).

- **Three root candidates, a lazy pass first.** The root tries the ranking's first three moves, singles and doubles alike, and no greedy probe gates them (see "Considered options"). Each candidate runs a lazy pass first: the children the plain path turned by a die are left aside, so its mass is a lower bound of the full proof's and a candidate under `MIN_FORCED_MASS` stops there. A candidate above it gets the full pass, and the better mass stands (`prover.ts`, `candidateMass`). The three share the 100-cell budget.
- **A die the simulator gives no price for stays unpriced.** A plain group whose draws differ in the faints or in the game's end, or whose turn drew one die more than once (a contact ability, a repeated Protect), is unpriced, and no proof runs through it (`endgame/children.ts`, `plainGroup`). Where the draws agree and the shares are known, the child carries the simulator's own chance, so a repeated Protect splits a cell by the chance it passes to `randomChance`.
- **A proof below mass 1 stays visible** at or above `MIN_FORCED_MASS`, for a side that lost its game too, and the report, which still speaks from `SPOKEN_MASS`, names the share below mass 1 as the card does (`forcedQualifier` in `prose/phrases.ts`: "in 98% of the rolls").
- **Draw counts.** The plain path draws five seeds per damaging doubles pair and three in singles (`PLAIN_DRAWS`). The disclosed limit stays: a damage roll or a crit that does not decide a knockout is not priced, so a doubles proof can rest on it (user ruling of 10.11.2026).

The two rules together cost about 2.5 times the prover's time on the 163 positions of the lane's sample, which adds about 1.3 % to a doubles search. The card does not name a stall: no new `ForcedWinOpen` kind and no change to `summary.ts` (user ruling of 11.11.2026).

## Amended (round 89, #130)

The win paths ("How could <player> have won?") use the prover as their first step and give it three options beyond the budget. The defaults are the production path, so the score, the card and the report read as before.

- **`floor`** (`ProverExtras.floor`, default `MIN_FORCED_MASS`): the mass under which a candidate or an AND node stops. The win paths pass 0, so a proof that wins in a small share of the dice stays visible, and the step reports the best mass it found and the played choice's mass.
- **`plan`** (`ProverExtras.plan`, default off): when on, the prover records for each position it proves the own choice that proved it and the reply that gave the minimum mass (`ProofPlan`, `plans`, `planAt`). `candidateProof` proves one own choice at the root by itself, at the full pass, and reads the shared memo, so a later candidate reuses what the first proved. The step follows these plans to build the line that ends in the win.
- **`capped`** (a field of the prover): true once the states cap or the cells cap cut a branch. A cut by depth is the turns the request asked for and does not count. The step reads it to tell `stopped` (the budget ran out) from `none` (every candidate was proved and none wins).

A step passes the cap of `WIN_BUDGET` as both the states and the cells cap and the turns asked as the depth. The job that runs the step lives on a worker of its own (ADR 0025); the prover in the analysis keeps `PROVER_BUDGET` and its defaults.

## Amended (round 89, lane P)

Champions VGC 2682637901 turn 6 is a certain win by the game (Aqua Jet knocks the foe's last body out before it moves, and its Protect can only repeat while the PP last), and the prover read it at 7/12: each repeated Protect was a new position one level deeper, the paralysis die of the partner doubled every level, and the 100 shared cells ended after two. Lane P changed four rules of the plain cell; stage P3 adds a fifth, a sure-strike candidate, in its own block.

- **A repeated stall that changes nothing is a delay.** In a cell where a foe body that carries the stall volatile clicks a stalling move that rolls (`rollsStall`), a child is a delay when it did not end and its delay facts equal the parent's: the serialized board with HP kept and with these dropped: the foe's stall volatile, the PP of every slot, a slot's `used` flag, the last-move fields, the entry counters beyond 0 or 1 and the switch-in order, each of them only when no entry on the board reads it (`endgame/delay-facts.ts`; the entries come from the handlers of the battle's own Dex, a fallen body's entries only where a standing body may revive it). A child counts when the repeat held or when it failed and the foe still stands. The rule applies when every held child is a delay and the side's own moves keep more PP than the foe's stall PP (twice that against a foe whose ability has a PP-deduction handler: Pressure). The cell is then worth X = (the share-weighted mass of the other children) / (1 - the delays' share), and the proof names the stalling move (`ForcedWinProof.delay`, optional, ADR 0014).
- **Soundness.** By induction over the foe's stall PP. Let W be the least cell mass of the other replies at the parent; a delay child has the parent's facts, so the other replies meet the same boards there. A delay child is the parent with another stall counter and one stall PP less. The other children lose the volatile, so their positions do not depend on the counter, and their relative shares are the side's own dice. With no stall PP left the foe has no stall reply and the value is W; otherwise the cell is worth at least X(1 - d) + d min(W, X) >= min(W, X) for d the delay share, so the parent is worth at least min(W, X), the value the AND node takes. The hold chance never rises along a chain (1 in 3, then 1 in 9 and so on; Generations 3 and 4 stop at 1 in 8), and the chain is finite because PP is. The margin keeps the side's own choice legal along the chain. A held child that is no delay (a heal, a status, the partner's hit, a contact shield's damage, a stage raised) refuses the rule for the whole cell, and the hold stays a position of its own. `test/forced-win-delay-sweep.spec.ts` plays the rule against every stalling move that rolls in Generations 2 to 9 and the Champions mod, singles and doubles, and holds each stall that matters to the simulator's rollouts.
- **The split read.** A repeated stall whose draws split the end (the hold keeps the board, the failure ends the game) is unpriced on the plain path, because three singles draws or five doubles draws of a die that comes true one time in three disagree on the end with probability 18/27 and 210/243. `EndgameChildren.delaySplit` prices the cell through its recorded dice, from a draw that did not end, when the dice explain every draw and none repeated; the prover reads it only in a repeated-stall cell and only where the delay rule fires. This is the narrow form of pricing a split through its dice: no proof runs through a split without a delay, so #145's scene (the held Protect lets the partner hit) and 2628166702 turn 7 (Leftovers) stay without a proof, as the game has it.
- **The share of a flipped die.** A flipped branch is worth the chance of its die's other answer times the recorded chances of the other deciding dice that branch drew again (`FlippedTurn.drawn`). A failed second Protect that ends the game before Volcarona's paralysis die is rolled is worth 2/3, not 7/12. The shares stay disjoint events and sum to at most 1.
- **The lazy pass reads an ended child.** A turned child that ended costs no cell and no state, so the lazy pass reads it; the failed repeat of a split cell is such a child.

The share rule and the lazy read apply to every plain cell, not only to repeated stalls (ruling of the integrator, round 89: both give the simulator's own chance of a branch and can only raise a mass toward the rollouts); the lane's 163-position sample reads their effect apart from the delay's.

Measured on those 163 positions (154 of them held a proof at the base): the delay rule changed six proofs, all for the side that won its game. Three rose to mass 1 (0.963, 0.963 and 0.667) and three kept mass 1 in fewer turns (12 to 9, 9 to 4, 3 to 2). It removed and lowered no proof, added none, and left the 12 proofs of the side that lost as they were. The share rule and the lazy read in every cell raised one more proof (a Champions OU position, 0.972 to 1) and lowered none, so the limited and the general form agree except there. The cells drawn fell from 5,948 to 5,693 over the sample, and the prover time moved less than the spread of the machine (about 10 %, read in both run orders).
