# The static evaluation has one weight table and one K for each game family

Singles and doubles are equal citizens, and VGC counts on its own (user, 28.08.2026; on 09.10.2026 the user said that VGC will be a large audience of the tool). The static evaluation therefore does not share one weight vector. `featureWeights(doubles, ruleset)` picks one of five tables: singles, Doubles OU, VGC doubles, Champions singles and Champions doubles. The logistic that turns a score into a win probability has one phase-aware K entry for each of the five families (`WINPROB_K_OF`; K is the steepness of the curve from score to win chance). The entries came from the fit of 2026-08-09 (singles, Doubles OU) and round 67 (Champions doubles); the family-wise refit of round 74 (T163) adopted none, so VGC repeats the Doubles OU entry and Champions singles the standard one. `DISPLAY_K` (with its late-game ramp) is a second stage for the displayed percentage. A table changes only through the calibration gate: the `EVAL_FIT=1` harness fits on the manifest-pinned corpus (`fit-corpus-manifest.json`) with cluster-bootstrap errors. The tables differ in how they were made: Champions OU and Doubles OU carry fitted weights (round 65, gate 09.10.2026: Doubles OU adopted its fit although it passed the pre-registered cross-check in only 17 of 20 seeds, where the rule asked for 20), SV VGC keeps the hand table with its own ruleset, Champions VGC has its own weights (round 66). New features sit at weight 0 until a fit adopts them. Measuring follows the split: every measurement counts doubles and VGC separately, and doubles verdicts read the bank together with the Doubles OU and VGC holdouts (D26), because the bank alone could not see a doubles gain. Doubles choices are combined two-slot options (`move thunderbolt 1, switch 3`), cut to `RESTRICT_K_DOUBLES` combined options per side at the root, because the raw product reaches hundreds per side and a full matrix would take minutes (the doubles design estimated about 90 s for 10,000 to 40,000 cells).

## Considered options

- Weights per format fitted freely (T50): Singles lost 120 bp (basis points of Brier score) and Doubles 107 bp out of sample, and only shrunk Doubles weights survived. The triage discarded it because the gain sat an order of magnitude below the bank's resolution.
- K per format, per source and per generation: they lost 6.2, 2.2 and 1.3 bp out of sample, an isotonic form lost 15 bp, and round 48 adopted no K because the verdict thresholds are measured in win percent and so move with K (T40 recorded that K per mode, phase and game type, and per rating band, all lost; the family split came later, in round 74).
- One shared table with game-type terms: the sources record no such variant as weighed. They record the fitting problem that led to the split: until round 65 a better doubles fit did not read better on the check, as it did in singles, and the round changed the method to "learn like the app, test on fresh games".
- A doubles K that was 36 bp better on the bank (T52): worse on the Doubles OU holdout, so it was never adopted; round 74 sets a per-family adoption rule.
- Singles first and doubles later: rejected by the user on 28.08.2026 after round 20 shipped T0 branching with a singles-only guard.

## Consequences

Every new feature term needs a value in five tables, and Champions OU, the smallest family, fits on little data. Each table is written out in full, so a refit of one never moves another. A refit runs only where the bank reads harm after a game-correct change (see ADR 0006).
