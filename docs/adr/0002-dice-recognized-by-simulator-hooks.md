# The dice module names a die by where the simulator draws it

The simulator's PRNG calls carry no label: an accuracy check, a confusion check and a secondary effect can all arrive as a draw out of 100. The forced-dice module names a die by two rules, in this order. First, by the hook the simulator runs right before the draw: the `Accuracy` event, `ModifyCritRatio`, `ModifySecondaries` for each target, the handler of a status, ability or item (`BeforeMove`, `DamagingHit`, `Damage`, `FractionalPriority`, `Residual`), and `battle.randomizer` for the damage roll; a hook that runs twice before one draw (Gen 2's `Accuracy`) names one die. Second, where no hook runs, by the simulator method that encloses the draw together with the draw's form: the hit count in the multi-hit loop, the speed tie in the queue's shuffle, the self effect in `selfDrops`, and in Gens 1 and 2 the crit and the damage roll in `getDamage` and the secondary in `tryMoveHit` after the hook `Hit`. The numbers of a call decide nothing on their own. The module answers a die with a number from its range, so the simulator's own comparison turns the number into the result. Until round 86 the engine's scripted dice told draws by the numbers of the call and read a paralysis, infatuation or confusion check as the move's own roll; since T299 they ask the module (see Consequences).

The hook probe of round 82 (step A1, 31.10.2026) found the seven kinds without a hook; the user approved the second rule at the gate after it ("3a").

## Considered options

- The numbers of the call (`ScriptedPRNG` and `PairPRNG` until T299): they collide with status checks in every generation, with the Champions paralysis and thaw, and they miss the 256ths of Gens 1 and 2.
- `Battle#forceRandomChance`: one answer for every chance of the battle, no draw consumed, so later draws shift; it covers neither damage rolls nor secondary effects.
- Rewriting move data (a secondary's chance set to 0 or 100): it writes the live move and cannot tell one target or one hit from another.
- Leaving the seven kinds without a hook out of the feature: the hit count and the speed tie are among the dice players ask about most.

## Consequences

Round 86 (T299) moved the tree's scripted dice onto the module. `ScriptedPRNG` (`forward/scripted-prng.ts`) and `PairPRNG` (`pair/prng.ts`) ask a `DrawNamer` (`dice/namer.ts`) before they answer: one `watchBattle` per battle, `recognize` and `settle` once per call of the simulator, also for a draw the PRNG answers itself, because a skipped call would leave the watch's queue of secondary entries behind the stream. A script answers only the move's own accuracy, crit and damage roll (keyed `${side}:${moveId}`) and, since T305, its secondary dice by effect id; `scriptRoll` (`dice/answer.ts`) maps the script's roll to the raw value of the generation, the 256ths of Gens 1 and 2 included. The numbers of a call decide nothing. A status check of the attacker (full paralysis, infatuation, confusion, the thaw) draws from the seed. An answer consumes no draw, as before.

The watch's wrappers (`dice/watch.ts`) keep the owner's own descriptor attributes: the mods of Gens 1 to 3 and Champions own some script calls (`tryMoveHit` and others) as enumerable properties of `battle.actions`, and a non-enumerable wrapper would drop the key from a copy, which would then play the base class's method. `release()` puts the battle back as it was (`toPosition` and `pair/draw.ts` call it), so a position leaves the PRNG without wrappers. Three specs hold it: `dice-namer.spec.ts` (the namer's names equal the recording PRNG's keys and counts in every generation and mod, singles and doubles), `scripted-dice-parity.spec.ts` (a script for a move that is not played equals the plain PRNG in log, end seed, serialization and keys, and the new class equals a frozen copy of the old one on the pinned scenes) and the 196 rows of `scripted-dice-status.spec.ts`, 88 of which were expected failures before. The cost is one watch per scripted fork: scripted forks are 0.2 % of the singles tree's forks and pair forks 0.7 % (Doubles OU) and 0.9 % (VGC).
