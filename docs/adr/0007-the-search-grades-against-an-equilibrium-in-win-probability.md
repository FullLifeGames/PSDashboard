# The search solves each turn as a matrix game in win-probability units and grades against its equilibrium

A turn in Pokémon is a simultaneous choice, so the best move depends on what the opponent picks. The first design (31.07.2026) ranked choices by worst case (maximin), showed the midpoint of the two sides' guarantees and named mixed-strategy output a non-goal. The scoring overhaul (04.08.2026) changed that. `solveMatrixGame` runs deterministic regret-matching self-play (`EQUILIBRIUM_ITERATIONS`) on the choice matrix, and the ranking sorts by the equilibrium value, with the worst case as the tie break. Regret is how much win probability a choice gave up against the best choice, when the opponent plays the mixture that is hardest to beat: the equilibrium value of the best choice minus the value of the played one. The score is the game value, clamped into the maximin interval, and the maximin floor stays as the "safe line" column. The reason the plan of 04.08.2026 records: the floor calls a read (a risky but reasonable play) a misplay, with a floor regret of 0.3 against an equilibrium regret of 0.05, below the verdict threshold. No gate answer for this change is recorded. Three days later (07.08.2026) the long-term design moved the solve into win-probability units: cell values pass through the fitted `winProbability` at the leaf (`wpUnits`) before the solver runs, so the mix, the values and the regret all live in win-probability units. The reason: expectation does not commute through a sigmoid. Below 50 % variance raises the true win probability and above 50 % it lowers it, so a solve in score units is risk-averse when the losing side needs risk. A player who is behind now sees high-variance lines outrank slow losses, and a player who is ahead sees consolidation outrank giving outs. The verdict tiers sit on the equilibrium regret in win-probability units (`TIER_THRESHOLDS`: inaccuracy at a 5 point loss of win probability, mistake at 10, blunder at 20, half of Lichess's bands); a unit of 0.1 means 5 points because the units run from -1 to +1. The tree mode ranks its root with the same solve.

Verdicts grade the decision by expectation and never by the outcome. Luck is split off as a separate chance part of each swing; the narrative text, the odds language and the cumulation of streaks are render-time and never change a verdict; later evidence can acquit a verdict (sensitivity, verification) and never add blame. The design of 07.08.2026 states that results-oriented grading "remains rejected". The sources record no argument beyond that.

## Considered options

- Maximin only (the first design): rejected on the floor-regret example above. The floor stays as the safe line.
- Grading from the visit counts of the Monte-Carlo trees: rejected in the plan of 04.08.2026 because visit counts do not converge to Nash. The tree's value is a visit mean and never a solved mixture.
- Solving in score units: rejected on the sigmoid argument above. The deeper layer the design named (win probabilities averaged over roll groups inside a cell) arrived with round 6 as outcome-class pricing of boundary cells (see ADR 0009).
- Folding luck into the skill grade: an anti-goal of the plan of 04.08.2026.

## Consequences

Every value, tier threshold, cache entry and pin depends on the unit, so a change of the mapping is a score-touching change with a cache bump and a refit of K (see ADR 0019). The top-ranked move is no longer the one with the highest floor. A regret of at least `REGRET_THRESHOLD` marks a decision problem, and a chance part of at least `CHANCE_THRESHOLD` marks a chance swing; both equal the mistake tier.
